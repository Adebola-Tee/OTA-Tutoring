# IGCSE Remark vs Resit in Hong Kong 2026: Which Should You Choose?

IGCSE results are out, and for some students in Hong Kong, the biggest question now is simple:

**Should I request a remark, or should I resit the exam?**

Both options can make sense, but they solve different problems.

A remark may be worth considering if the result looks unusually low compared with the student's normal performance. A resit is usually more useful when the student genuinely struggled with the subject and needs time to improve.

Here is how to decide.

## What does an IGCSE remark mean?

What students commonly call a **remark** is usually known officially as a **review of marking**.

The examination board checks whether the original marking was carried out correctly. It is not simply a request to award extra marks.

A review may be worth discussing when:

- the result is much lower than expected;
- mock results were consistently stronger;
- the student is very close to the next grade boundary;
- the grade affects A Level, IB or sixth-form entry;
- a teacher believes the result should be reviewed.

Before requesting one, speak to the school's examinations officer and check the student's marks and grade boundaries.

## When is a resit the better choice?

A resit makes more sense when the result reflects a genuine weakness in the subject.

For example, if a student struggled throughout IGCSE Mathematics with algebra, fractions or problem-solving, a remark is unlikely to fix the underlying issue.

The student may need time to:

- rebuild weak topics;
- improve exam technique;
- practise timed questions;
- correct recurring mistakes;
- prepare properly for another attempt.

The most important question is therefore:

**Why was the grade low?**

## Remark vs resit: quick comparison

| Situation                                  | Better option                        |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------ |
| Result is unexpectedly low                 | Consider a remark                    |
| Student narrowly missed an important grade | Discuss a remark                     |
| Student struggled throughout the course    | Consider a resit                     |
| Major knowledge gaps remain                | Resit with targeted preparation      |
| Grade affects the next course              | Discuss both options with the school |

There is no automatic answer.

The right decision depends on the student's marks, previous performance and future academic plans.

## Can a grade change after a remark?

A review of marking does not guarantee a higher grade.

That is why families should avoid requesting one simply because they are disappointed with the result.

Speak with the school first and establish whether there is a genuine reason to question the marking.

## Do not resit without fixing the real problem

Resitting the same examination with the same preparation can lead to the same result.

Before beginning another round of revision, identify what actually went wrong.

Was it:

- weak subject knowledge?
- poor timing?
- careless mistakes?
- misunderstanding exam questions?
- weak written explanations?
- insufficient practice?

Once the cause is clear, preparation becomes much more focused.

## What should parents do after IGCSE results?

Before choosing a remark or resit:

1. Check the student's mark and grade boundary.
2. Compare the result with previous school performance.
3. Speak to the subject teacher or examinations officer.
4. Check whether the grade affects A Level, IB or sixth-form entry.
5. Identify whether the problem was marking or academic performance.
6. Then decide whether a review, resit or simply moving forward makes the most sense.

Sometimes, the best decision is neither a remark nor a resit.

If the student already meets the requirements for their next programme, focusing on the new academic year may be far more valuable.

## Need help deciding what comes next?

A disappointing IGCSE result should lead to a plan, not a rushed decision.

If the grade has exposed gaps in Mathematics, Science, Computer Science or another subject, addressing them now can make the transition into A Levels, IB or the next school year much smoother.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an IGCSE remark the same as a review of marking?

“Remark” is the common term, while examination boards usually call the process a **review of marking**.

### Is a remark better than a resit?

Not necessarily. A remark is more appropriate when there is reason to question the marking. A resit is usually better when the student needs to improve their knowledge or exam performance.

### Should I resit an IGCSE just because I dislike my grade?

No. First check whether the grade affects the student's next academic step and understand why the result was lower than expected.

## The key question

Before choosing between an **IGCSE remark and a resit**, ask:

**Does the result look wrong, or does the student need to improve?**

That answer usually makes the next step much clearer.
