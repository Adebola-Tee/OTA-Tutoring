If you received **IGCSE results in Hong Kong in 2026**, you may be comparing your grades with students who took a different exam board and wondering whether Cambridge and Edexcel results work in the same way.

Both **Cambridge IGCSE** and **Pearson Edexcel International GCSE** are widely recognised international qualifications, but their result release dates, grading systems, grade boundaries and post-results processes are not identical.

Understanding those differences is particularly useful if you are considering a **remark, review or IGCSE retake in Hong Kong**.

## Cambridge vs Edexcel IGCSE Results in Hong Kong

The biggest mistake students make is assuming that a Cambridge grade and an Edexcel grade can always be compared simply by looking at the number or letter printed on the result.

They cannot.

The two exam boards run separate qualifications, set separate papers and determine their own grade boundaries for each examination series.

That means students should judge their result against the requirements of **their own syllabus and exam board**, rather than comparing raw marks with friends who took a different qualification.

## When Were Cambridge IGCSE Results Released in 2026?

Cambridge International released results for the **June 2026 Cambridge IGCSE examination series on 18 August 2026**.

Students normally receive their results through their school or examination centre. Some schools may also provide access to Cambridge's Candidate Results website.

If you took Cambridge IGCSEs in Hong Kong and cannot access your detailed results, your school's exams officer should be your first point of contact.

## When Were Edexcel International GCSE Results Released in 2026?

Pearson released **June 2026 Edexcel International GCSE results on 20 August 2026**.

That means Cambridge IGCSE students received their June-series results two days earlier than Edexcel International GCSE students in 2026.

The difference in results day does not mean that one qualification is easier, harder or more valuable than the other.

It is simply part of each exam board's own examination timetable.

## Do Cambridge and Edexcel Use the Same IGCSE Grades?

Not necessarily.

Pearson Edexcel International GCSE qualifications generally use the **9–1 grading scale**, with Grade 9 representing the highest performance.

Cambridge IGCSE qualifications may use either **A*–G or 9–1**, depending on the syllabus and grading option offered.

This is one reason students in Hong Kong should be careful when comparing grades across schools.

A Cambridge student with an A*, for example, and an Edexcel student with a Grade 9 have completed different qualifications with different assessment structures and grade boundaries.

Universities and schools familiar with international qualifications understand these grading systems.

## Are Cambridge and Edexcel Grade Boundaries the Same?

No.

Cambridge and Pearson calculate grade boundaries independently for their own qualifications.

A particular percentage in one Edexcel International GCSE subject does not automatically produce the same grade as the identical percentage in a Cambridge IGCSE subject.

Grade boundaries can also change between examination series.

The difficulty of individual papers may vary, so exam boards set boundaries after considering candidate performance and the standard required for each grade.

For that reason, students should avoid statements such as:

**"I got 70%, so I should definitely have received an A."**

The correct question is:

**What was the grade boundary for my exact syllabus, paper and examination series?**

## How Do Edexcel IGCSE Grade Boundaries Work?

Pearson publishes grade boundaries after each examination series.

For the June 2026 International GCSE series, Pearson published separate **International GCSE 9–1 grade boundaries** alongside results.

Students should check the exact subject and qualification code rather than relying on unofficial grade-boundary predictions found online.

If your result is very close to the next grade, knowing the official boundary can help you decide whether requesting a review of marking is worth investigating.

## What Should Cambridge IGCSE Students Check After Results?

If your Cambridge IGCSE grade is lower than expected, do not immediately assume that you need to retake the entire subject.

Start by asking your school for more detailed result information.

You should try to understand:

- your component marks
- your overall result
- the grade boundary for your syllabus
- whether one component significantly reduced your final grade
- what post-results services are available
- the deadline for submitting an enquiry about results

This will help you make a more informed decision.

## What Should Edexcel IGCSE Students Check After Results?

Pearson Edexcel students should take a similar approach.

For the June 2026 International GCSE series, Pearson's main deadline for reviews of marking and moderation is **24 September 2026**.

That means Hong Kong students considering an Edexcel review should speak to their school or examination centre promptly rather than waiting until the end of September.

You may also be able to request access to your examination scripts through your centre.

Seeing your marked work can be extremely useful when deciding whether a review is sensible or whether a retake would be more beneficial.

## Cambridge vs Edexcel IGCSE Remark: Is the Process the Same?

The terminology and procedures are slightly different.

Cambridge commonly refers to its post-results process as **enquiries about results**.

Pearson uses **reviews of marking and moderation** as part of its post-results services.

In both cases, however, the basic principle is similar: your school or examination centre requests an official post-results service from the exam board.

Students normally do not submit these requests independently.

Your exams officer can explain which service is appropriate, what it costs and what the deadline is.

## When Should You Consider an IGCSE Remark in Hong Kong?

A remark or review may be worth investigating when:

- you are only a few marks below the next grade
- your result is unexpectedly different from your school performance
- your teacher believes the result deserves closer examination
- one component appears unusually low
- the higher grade is important for sixth form or university progression

However, a review does not guarantee that your result will increase.

Students should look at the actual marks and grade boundaries before making the decision.

## When Is an IGCSE Retake Better?

A retake can be a better option if there is a significant gap between your current grade and the result you need.

It may also make sense if:

- you were underprepared for the original exam
- you struggled with several parts of the syllabus
- you had poor exam technique
- you ran out of time
- you made repeated avoidable mistakes
- you know you could perform substantially better after focused preparation

A retake gives you the opportunity to improve your actual performance rather than relying on your existing paper being reviewed.

## Is Cambridge IGCSE Harder Than Edexcel IGCSE?

There is no reliable universal answer.

Cambridge and Edexcel structure subjects differently, and students may find one style more comfortable than the other.

For example, differences may appear in:

- question wording
- syllabus organisation
- assessment structure
- calculator or non-calculator requirements in some subjects
- coursework or practical components
- the balance between straightforward and problem-solving questions

The exam board that feels easier for one student may feel more difficult for another.

The important question is not simply **"Which exam board is easier?"**

It is whether the student has been properly prepared for the specification they are actually taking.

## Does One IGCSE Exam Board Have Better Results?

Neither Cambridge nor Edexcel automatically gives students better grades.

Your final result depends on your performance against the standard required by your qualification.

Schools in Hong Kong may choose Cambridge, Pearson Edexcel or a combination of international examination boards depending on their curriculum.

Students should therefore focus on understanding their own specification rather than assuming that changing boards automatically produces higher grades.

## Can You Retake Cambridge or Edexcel IGCSEs in Hong Kong?

Retake opportunities depend on the subject, examination board and examination series.

Cambridge offers many IGCSE subjects in its October/November series, while Pearson International GCSE subjects may have different available examination windows depending on the qualification.

If you are considering an IGCSE retake in Hong Kong, ask your school or examination centre:

- whether your subject is available in the next series
- the registration deadline
- whether you can retake individual components or must retake the qualification
- the examination fee
- when the new result will be released

Do this before building your revision schedule.

## What If Your IGCSE Result Was Lower Than Expected?

First, do not make the decision based only on emotion.

Collect the information.

Find out:

1. your exact mark
2. the grade boundary
3. how far you were from the next grade
4. whether your exam board offers an appropriate review
5. when the post-results deadline is
6. whether a retake is available soon enough for your academic plans

Then decide.

A student who missed the next grade by one mark may have a very different decision from someone who needs to improve by an entire grade or more.

## How to Prepare for an IGCSE Retake in Hong Kong

If you decide to retake, your revision should begin with your previous performance.

Do not simply restart the textbook.

Identify:

- topics where you consistently lost marks
- questions you misunderstood
- gaps in subject knowledge
- weak examination technique
- timing problems
- careless mistakes

Then combine targeted revision with official past-paper practice.

The closer you get to the examination, the more frequently you should practise under timed conditions.

## Cambridge vs Edexcel IGCSE Results: Which Is Better for Hong Kong Students?

Neither result system is automatically better.

Cambridge and Pearson Edexcel are both established international examination boards, and their qualifications are used by schools and students around the world.

The key differences students need to understand after results day are:

- when results are released
- which grading scale applies to their qualification
- how their grade boundaries were determined
- what post-results services are available
- whether and when they can retake the subject

Those practical differences matter far more after results day than simply asking which exam board is more difficult.
