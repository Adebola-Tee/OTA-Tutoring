When a parent asks for an Edexcel IGCSE Maths tutor, the first useful detail is the full course name. Pearson offers different qualifications and assessment routes. A tutor who begins with the correct specification can make preparation far more relevant than someone selecting questions only because they say Edexcel.

## Check the course and route with school

Ask whether your child follows International GCSE Mathematics A and confirm the specification code and tier. For a linear route, assessment happens at the end of the course; a modular route divides assessment into units. Those arrangements affect how preparation is organised.

Pearson's [International GCSE Mathematics A page](https://qualifications.pearson.com/en/qualifications/edexcel-international-gcses/international-gcse-mathematics-a-2016.html) provides the linear qualification information. If your school uses modular assessment, supply its course details too. A tutor should check the relevant materials and deadlines with the learner rather than apply a generic timetable.

Do not confuse International GCSE with the UK GCSE simply because both are offered by Pearson Edexcel. Similar mathematics does not make the qualifications identical.

## Ask how calculator work is taught

Calculator access does not remove the need to understand expressions, estimate and present a method. Consider a student asked to calculate 3.6 divided by 0.12. A calculator may produce 30, but the learner should still recognise why the result is larger than 3.6: the divisor is less than one.

Ask how the tutor investigates an implausible answer or a mistyped expression. Good habits include recording the setup, checking the order of operations and keeping sufficient precision until the final answer.

A lesson that only demonstrates button sequences may leave the child unable to adapt when the context changes. The student should explain what the result means as well as obtain it.

## Look for teaching based on an actual attempt

A wrong graph answer could come from misreading a scale, confusing coordinates or misunderstanding gradient. These need different follow-up tasks. Ask the tutor to explain which evidence would distinguish them.

Our article on [graphs and gradient in IGCSE Maths](online-igcse-maths-tutor-kuwait-graphs-gradient.html) gives a concrete example of that teaching focus. The mathematics is relevant beyond Kuwait; the student's own qualification and school sequence should determine the resources used.

Starting with the first chapter of a textbook is not always efficient. A useful plan can repair a missing prerequisite while keeping the learner connected with current school topics.

## Check whether the student participates

The tutor needs to see written working, hear explanations and allow time for independent attempts. Ask how this works online, including whether paper can be shown clearly or another suitable method used.

Watching a fluent teacher solve a hard question is different from deciding how to begin alone. A lesson should include the second experience too. Feedback can then respond to the student's reasoning rather than merely confirm the final answer.

For a more confident learner, ask how the challenge increases. Comparing methods or explaining why an answer is impossible can be more useful than adding another page of familiar calculations.

## Agree what happens after the first lesson

Discuss a first priority and a review point. Progress might mean interpreting a graph accurately, solving a changed algebra problem or making fewer repeated rounding errors. Expect evidence from unfamiliar work alongside school assessments, not a guaranteed grade increase.

Before booking, clarify the session duration, frequency, fee and payment arrangements. Ask what practice and feedback the proposed package includes.
