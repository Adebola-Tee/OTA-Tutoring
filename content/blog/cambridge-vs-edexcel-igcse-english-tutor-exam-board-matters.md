A child can work hard at English and still feel that the marks do not reflect their ability. For parents, the difficult part is knowing whether to demand more practice, revisit the basics or find specialist support. With **matching teaching and resources to the correct specification and paper demands**, the answer should begin with evidence rather than guesswork.

## What is probably going wrong?

The clearest starting diagnosis is that generic IGCSE preparation can train the wrong question structures, timings or assessment priorities. This can produce answers that sound reasonable but do not consistently satisfy the task.

English marks are awarded for visible decisions. An examiner cannot reward an idea that remains in the student’s head. The response must select the right detail, organise it around the question and explain it with enough precision.

The official [Cambridge IGCSE First Language English 0500 page](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-igcse-first-language-english-0500/) confirms that students are expected to handle selecting information, analysing language, reshaping source material and writing accurately for a defined purpose. That is why general fluency or simply completing more papers does not always solve the problem.

## What should be fixed first?

Begin with one marked script. Do not look only at the grade. Label each lost mark as one of the following:

- misunderstanding the question or command word
- weak selection of evidence or content
- explanation that stops too early
- poor organisation or paragraph control
- inaccurate or limited expression
- timing, omission or unfinished work

Then count the pattern. The first practical change should be to confirm the full qualification title and syllabus code before lessons begin. A narrow correction repeated properly is more valuable than another broad revision session.

## What productive practice looks like

A useful study cycle has five parts:

1. Model the skill using one strong example.
2. Make the thinking visible with a short plan or annotation.
3. Complete one focused task without help.
4. Correct the precise weakness, not the entire answer blindly.
5. Retest the same skill later on an unfamiliar question.

The final step matters. Students often understand feedback while the teacher is beside them, then repeat the error independently. A fresh task shows whether the method has transferred.

## How parents can help without teaching the course

Ask questions that reveal the process: “What exactly did this question require?”, “Which sentence earns the mark?” and “What will you do differently next time?” These questions are more useful than asking how many hours the child studied.

Keep a simple weekly record of one priority, one timed task and one recurring error. Progress may first appear as better completion, clearer paragraphs or fewer repeated mistakes before the overall grade rises.
