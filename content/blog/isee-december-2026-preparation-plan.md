Preparing for an ISEE sitting in December 2026 starts with the family's actual booking. There is no single December test day that applies to every student. Test locations and delivery options offer different appointments, and a school's application deadline is a separate date that also needs checking.

The [official ISEE overview](https://www.admission.org/assessments/isee/about) places December in the Winter testing season. Students may test once per season. Confirm your booked date, level and each school's score requirements before deciding whether a future sitting fits the application timetable.

## Put four details at the top of the plan

Record the test date, entry grade, test format and school deadline. The entry grade determines the relevant level; current grade alone is not enough. Keep the booking confirmation available so the family can check instructions rather than rely on a date remembered from a conversation.

For families seeking Maths support, [our online ISEE tutoring guide](online-isee-math-tutor-upper-level.html) explains what to bring to a consultation. Targeted lessons work best when there is an actual practice attempt to review, rather than only a general request to improve everything.

## Use the first week to diagnose

Begin with appropriate practice for the confirmed level. Review the working as well as the number correct. Separate missing knowledge from misunderstood wording and decisions made too slowly. A learner who understands an explanation afterwards may still need practice recognising when to use it.

Keep a short error record. Write the mathematical issue and a next action, such as comparing fractions using a common denominator. Avoid a label like careless unless you can identify what the student should do differently next time.

## Spend the middle weeks repairing a small number of gaps

Choose priorities from the diagnostic. A tutor might work on ratio, algebra and geometry for one student, while another needs arithmetic accuracy and interpreting word problems. Leave time for the learner to try corrected skills independently between sessions.

Here is an original example. A group contains 24 students, and three eighths choose an activity. Nine choose it because 24 ÷ 8 × 3 = 9. Now change the information: nine students represent three eighths of the group. The full group is 9 ÷ 3 × 8 = 24.

Ask the learner why dividing by eight is useful in one version but not the first step in the other. Understanding what a fraction describes matters more than copying a remembered operation. Revisit the relationship later in a different context to check retention.

## Add timing after the method makes sense

Timed practice should reveal which decisions remain slow. Start with a small mixed set, review it and choose a specific adjustment. For example, a student might need to identify questions to return to rather than spend several minutes forcing one calculation.

Do not compare results from unrelated practice resources as though they were identical official scores. Track the same underlying skills and the amount of prompting needed. A better explanation and a more independent attempt are useful signs even before a larger practice result changes.

## Keep other ISEE sections in the calendar

A Maths plan does not replace preparation for the rest of the assessment. Coordinate reading, verbal work and writing with the family's wider plan. The [official test instructions](https://www.admission.org/assessments/isee/instructions/in-person-online) describe the sections and timing for the relevant level and format.

Avoid filling every available evening with a full practice test. A schedule needs correction time, other schoolwork and rest. A learner who repeats papers without reviewing recurring mistakes can work hard without changing the habits that cause difficulty.

## Use the final week to rehearse

Check the provider's current instructions, identification requirements and any approved accommodations. Online at home and in person arrangements differ, so use instructions for the booked delivery option. Prepare equipment or technical requirements early enough to resolve questions with the provider.

Reduce new content as test day approaches. Review a manageable set of corrections, rehearse pacing and protect sleep. A final lesson can clarify a persistent decision; it should not introduce an overwhelming replacement for the entire preparation routine.
