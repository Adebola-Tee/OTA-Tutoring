A weekly lesson can provide a useful rhythm for IGCSE Maths. Some learners need more frequent teaching; others need a narrower priority and better practice between appointments. The right frequency cannot be read from a predicted grade alone.

Start with what the sessions need to change. At OTA Learning Studio, our [free parent consultation](#consultation) lets you discuss the course, current difficulties and realistic practice time before we explain paid Google Meet lessons and class packages.

## Identify whether the problem is broad or specific

A student may be secure across most topics but repeatedly lose marks on rearranging equations. Another may struggle whenever fractions, negatives or proportional relationships appear. These starting points call for different amounts and sequences of teaching.

Take two or three recent attempts and ask where the first uncertainty occurs. If algebra breaks down because fraction arithmetic is weak, scheduling more advanced algebra practice alone will not solve the prerequisite problem.

The initial plan should name a priority such as forming a ratio equation or interpreting a graph scale. A general promise to improve Maths gives little basis for deciding how many sessions are worthwhile.

## Account for the actual assessment route

Confirm the board, tier and exam session with school. A modular learner may be preparing selected units while a linear candidate is preparing the whole qualification. Ask what current school teaching covers and what revision needs to revisit.

The [Pearson modular Mathematics A overview](https://qualifications.pearson.com/en/qualifications/edexcel-international-gcses/mathematics-a-2024-modular.html) explains that route. It should not be assumed to apply to every Edexcel student. Cambridge and other qualifications have their own arrangements.

An approaching exam creates a deadline, but does not make every possible lesson useful. Set priorities from the work and available preparation time, rather than buying an intensive schedule solely because the date feels close.

## Leave a gap for independent work

After a lesson on percentage change, a learner might explain why an increase from 50 to 60 is 20%. Later, they should tackle an unfamiliar decrease or reverse percentage without being told the method.

That attempt tells the tutor whether the explanation has transferred. If sessions are scheduled so closely that the student never tries work alone, useful evidence can be missed. More contact should have a reason, such as separating prerequisite repair from current school demands.

Our [IGCSE lesson guide](online-igcse-maths-lesson.html) shows how explanation and independent attempts can fit together. The schedule needs room for both, alongside the student's other subjects and rest.

## Consider a starting plan rather than a permanent rule

For a manageable, specific difficulty, one regular session with achievable practice can be a reasonable arrangement to discuss. For persistent gaps across several areas, additional sessions may help create enough teaching time. These are possibilities, not universal prescriptions.

Ask what each appointment would be for. If a second session is proposed, would it revisit independent work, teach a separate priority or connect current topics? An explanation based only on more hours means better grades is insufficient.

Agree a review point. Look for changed performance on fresh tasks, clearer explanations and less prompting. If those changes do not appear, investigate the teaching priority and practice before simply increasing frequency again.

## Book a schedule the learner can sustain

Share the school timetable, other commitments and time zone. Check lesson duration, the number of sessions in a package, fees and arrangements for missed dates. Confirm these details before paid lessons begin.

Bring a recent question to the consultation, even if you are unsure what the difficulty is called. We can discuss a sensible starting focus and available support. The aim is a rhythm that helps your child use more mathematics independently, not a crowded week of appointments that leaves no space to put the learning into practice.
