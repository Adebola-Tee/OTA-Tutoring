If your Year 6 child is already anxious about SATs Maths, October is a good time to find the cause rather than start a daily test paper. A pupil may know multiplication facts yet misread a multi-step question. Another may understand the story but struggle with fraction operations. Online tuition should respond to the actual gap.

The government's [2027 primary assessment dates](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/primary-assessments-future-dates) put KS2 mathematics papers on 12 and 13 May 2027. That leaves room to build knowledge carefully in autumn, revisit it in winter and introduce more timed work nearer the tests. It does not require treating every lesson as an exam rehearsal now.

## Separate arithmetic from reasoning

Ask the child to attempt a small set of arithmetic questions and a few worded reasoning tasks. Note what they do without help. A score alone cannot show whether they forgot a fact, chose the wrong operation, made a place-value slip or did not understand the situation.

Consider an original question: “A school orders 6 boxes of 24 pencils. It gives 37 pencils to Year 5. How many remain?” The child needs to find 6 × 24 = 144, then 144 − 37 = 107. A pupil who calculates 6 + 24 − 37 has not understood the relationship between boxes and pencils. More subtraction questions will not fix that first step.

Ask them to draw or say what the boxes represent, then make a new version with 8 boxes of 15. If the strategy transfers, understanding is developing. If it does not, return to the meaning of multiplication before adding time pressure.

## Look for patterns in fractions and place value

Year 6 work often reveals an earlier idea that never became secure. If a child adds 1/4 + 1/2 and says 2/6, ask them to show both fractions on a number line or as equal parts of the same whole. They should see that 1/2 = 2/4, so the sum is 3/4. A visual explanation can be followed by an unfamiliar problem to check independence.

Place value errors can be equally persistent. When multiplying 0.6 by 10, the result is 6, not 0.60. Have the child explain the size of the answer rather than relying only on a remembered “move the decimal” rule. Make sure the tutor checks explanation as well as the final number.

## What a useful online lesson looks like

An online Year 6 Maths tutor should let the child do the working. On Google Meet, a shared whiteboard or paper held to the camera can help, but the adult must not supply every next step. A good session may begin with five minutes of retrieval, spend most of the time on one important misconception and end with two fresh questions attempted with less prompting.

Homework need not be long. A short mix of today's skill and a previously learned one tells you more than pages of repeated questions. Ask the tutor how they will share progress: for example, fewer prompts needed to start a word problem and a correct method on a new question a week later.
