If your child attends an international school in Japan, searching for an **online Science tutor** can be surprisingly difficult. “Science” could mean an integrated middle-school course, Cambridge IGCSE Biology, Chemistry and Physics, or an IB programme. Before asking about lesson times, ask whether the tutor can work from the student's actual course.

Send a unit outline, recent lab or test question, and the school's assessment guidance if available. Those materials help us distinguish a difficult topic from a difficulty explaining scientific ideas in English.

## Start with the curriculum, not an age label

Two students of the same age may study quite different topics. The [IB Middle Years Programme curriculum](https://ibo.org/programmes/middle-years-programme/curriculum/) includes sciences among its subject groups, while Cambridge IGCSE uses subject-specific syllabuses. The school's course name, level and current unit matter more than the generic label “Year 8 Science.”

We would ask the learner to explain a recent question: What was observed? What scientific idea explains it? Which detail in the data supports that claim? If they know the vocabulary but cannot connect evidence to a conclusion, a lesson on scientific reasoning may help more than another set of flashcards.

## What a practical online Science lesson can do

Online tutoring need not pretend to replace a school's laboratory. It can help a learner interpret a method, identify variables, read a graph, calculate with units and explain why a conclusion follows from results.

For example, if a learner says a plant grew more because it had more light, ask what else was kept the same and whether the data support that explanation. Then show a new table and let the student decide what conclusion is justified. That is the sort of transfer we want to see.

For Chemistry or Physics, a tutor can separate the scientific model from the Maths. A student might understand density yet make an error converting units. Finding that exact point prevents an entire lesson from repeating what they already know.

## Time zones and continuity

Agree on a regular lesson time in Japan Standard Time and a practical way to share school materials in advance. A short note after each lesson can name the topic, the skill practised and one task to try before the next session. The parent should know what progress is being checked, not just how many lessons were completed.

If the learner is already doing well, support can focus on explaining unfamiliar data or comparing possible scientific explanations. Stretch should remain relevant to their current course.

## Ask these questions before booking

- Which curriculum and course does the tutor need to follow?
- Will the student work with real data or school questions during the session?
- How will you tell a Science gap from a Maths or language gap?
- Can the lesson time work reliably in Japan?
