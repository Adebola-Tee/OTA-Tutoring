Yes. Students taking GCSE Mathematics, Physics and Combined Science in 2027 will receive formulae or equation sheets in the relevant exams. That is welcome news if memorising equations has felt overwhelming. It does not remove the need to understand when and how to use them.

The Department for Education's [2027 exam guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/what-people-working-in-schools-and-colleges-need-to-know-before-exams-and-assessments/what-people-working-in-schools-and-colleges-need-to-know-before-exams-and-assessments) says boards will publish the 2027 sheets in December 2026, provide clean copies with exam papers, and use the same sheets as in 2026. Check your subject, specification and exam board with your school before planning detailed revision.

## The sheet solves only one part of the question

Imagine a GCSE Physics problem: a cyclist travels 150 metres in 30 seconds. A student may find speed equals distance divided by time on the sheet. They still have to notice which numbers represent distance and time, calculate 150 ÷ 30 = 5, and write **5 metres per second**.

If the question gives 150 centimetres instead, the units need attention before the answer is stated in metres per second. A formula on a page cannot decide that for the learner. It also cannot tell them whether the result is plausible in the situation described.

Maths presents a similar challenge. A formula might be available, but the difficult part can be recognising the relevant shape, identifying its measurements, rearranging an expression or explaining why a method works. Students need to connect the words of a problem to the calculation.

For instance, if a question gives a triangle's base as 8 cm and height as 5 cm, the area is 20 square centimetres. A learner who simply multiplies 8 by 5 has chosen the wrong relationship, even though both numbers were correctly copied. Ask them to sketch the triangle and explain why its area is half that of the matching rectangle.

## Practise finding the right formula

When the official 2027 sheets are published, use the version for the learner's board. Until then, a teacher can use the corresponding 2026 material as a familiarisation aid, while checking for any board instructions.

Try three steps with a short question. First, hide the sheet and name the quantities in the problem. Second, open the sheet and locate a relationship involving those quantities. Third, close it and explain what each symbol means in this particular question.

Do not race to substitute numbers. If a student sees “power” in the question and picks the first equation containing the word, ask whether the information given supports that equation. A tutor can listen to the choice and see where the misunderstanding begins.

## Review errors by cause

Mark each calculation error as one of four types: wrong relationship, wrong substitution, arithmetic or unit error, or missing explanation. A page of correct answers after copying a model can hide a continuing problem with the first step.

Choose one error type for the next lesson. For example, practise three questions requiring a unit conversion, then a mixed question a week later without a reminder. Improvement means selecting and applying the method independently, not recognising an identical worked example.

## Use the news to make a better study plan

There is no need to spend October making decorative formula flashcards. Spend it learning the ideas behind the relationships, reading questions carefully and checking units. Once December's official sheet is available, practise with its layout so finding an equation becomes quick and familiar.
