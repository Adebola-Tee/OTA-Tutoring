When a child is falling behind in **HKDSE Chemistry**, starting from the newest school chapter may not solve the problem. Chemistry is cumulative: weak foundations in formulae, equations, particles or amounts of substances reappear inside later topics.

The first step is to locate the earliest gap that is still causing errors.

## Diagnose before restarting the course

Use a recent test, homework and classwork. Sort mistakes into these areas:

- chemical knowledge not understood
- symbols, formulae or equations insecure
- calculations and units
- practical, graph or data questions
- knowledge understood but not applied
- answers lacking precise chemical language

The largest pattern should guide the starting point.

The plan should also match the official [HKEAA Chemistry subject information and assessment framework](https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/en/hkdse/assessment/subject_information/category_a_subjects/chem/).

## Repair the foundations that unlock other topics

High-impact foundations often include:

- atomic structure and bonding
- writing and balancing equations
- the mole and reacting quantities
- acids, bases and salts
- redox and electrochemistry
- interpreting experimental evidence

Do not spend weeks rereading every page. Test each foundation briefly, then teach only what the evidence shows is weak.

## Use a learn–question–correct cycle

For each priority:

1. Review one small concept.
2. Explain it without notes.
3. Complete focused questions.
4. Mark and classify the errors.
5. Reattempt a similar question later.

This creates stronger progress than copying model answers or highlighting a textbook.

## Keep calculations and theory connected

Students sometimes treat calculations as a separate branch of Chemistry. In reality, selecting the correct calculation depends on understanding what the particles and reaction are doing.

If numerical questions are the main weakness, our guide to [HKDSE Chemistry calculations](https://otalearningstudio.com/blog/hkdse-chemistry-calculations-pulling-down-results.html) provides a more targeted next step.

## Make the weekly plan small enough to complete

A student who already feels behind does not need an impossible timetable. Choose two priority topics, one calculation skill and one short mixed review each week.

Track progress through unseen questions, not hours spent at a desk.
