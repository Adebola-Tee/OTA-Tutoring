A Cambridge IGCSE Maths tutor should ask which syllabus your child follows before suggesting a revision plan. “Cambridge Maths” is a starting description, not enough information to select the right questions or judge the level of support needed.

If you are comparing online lessons, look for someone who connects the qualification with your child's actual working. ## Confirm the entry and examination year

For Mathematics 0580, establish whether the school is entering Core or Extended. Also share the year of examination. Cambridge publishes syllabuses by examination year, so the most recently downloaded document is not automatically the correct one for your child.

The [official Mathematics 0580 page](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580/) sets out the current qualification. Under the 2025–2027 syllabus, candidates encounter both calculator and non-calculator assessment. Ask how the tutor prepares for those different demands without relying on a calculator for every step.

If the school uses another Cambridge mathematics code, say so. A good provider should welcome that detail and check suitability rather than assume all Cambridge papers are interchangeable.

## Listen to the explanation of one error

Suppose a student simplifies 12/18 by subtracting 6 from both numbers and obtains 6/12. The original fraction equals two thirds, while the proposed answer equals one half. The process changed the value.

A useful explanation connects simplification to dividing numerator and denominator by a common factor. The learner then needs a fresh example and a chance to explain why the value stays the same. Reciting cancel down may leave the underlying misunderstanding untouched.

This kind of discussion tells a parent more about teaching than a list of topics the tutor claims to cover. Ask how that explanation would change if the difficulty involved algebraic fractions instead.

## Expect different work for different starting points

A learner approaching a pass may need reliable number methods and clear reading of questions. A confident Extended student may need more demanding algebra, connected reasoning or better justification. Their lessons should not look identical.

Ask what evidence would guide the first few sessions. The answer might include recent schoolwork, a short independent task and the student's explanation of where they get stuck. A full paper is useful sometimes, but not every initial difficulty requires another long test.

Our [IGCSE lesson example](online-igcse-maths-lesson.html) shows how the student can remain active during the teaching. The same principle should apply whether the priority is repairing a foundation or stretching a secure skill.

## Ask how progress will be demonstrated

A completed worksheet can show participation. More independent work on unfamiliar questions gives better evidence that the explanation is becoming usable knowledge. Ask how the tutor revisits a skill after a gap and mixes it with other topics.

For example, a student who simplifies numerical fractions correctly should later recognise when simplification helps in a percentage or ratio problem. The teacher needs to check that transfer, not assume that one successful exercise settles the topic permanently.

Parents should receive an understandable account of the priority and what comes next. A target grade can guide ambition, but nobody can responsibly guarantee the final examination result from an initial conversation.

## Make the booking decision practical

Ask about lesson length, how written work is shared, practice between sessions and available times in your time zone. Agree fees and cancellation arrangements before booking. A friendly tutor still needs a purposeful plan; a technically strong tutor still needs explanations the learner can follow.
