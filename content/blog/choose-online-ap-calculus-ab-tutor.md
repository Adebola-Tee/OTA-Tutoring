An AP Calculus AB tutor should help a student explain what a calculation says, not only carry it out. Your child may differentiate accurately and still hesitate when a question presents a graph, a table or a quantity changing over time.

## Ask how different representations are connected

Suppose a quantity has rate of change r(t) = 2t for 0 ≤ t ≤ 3. Integrating over that interval gives 9. The student should explain that this is the net change in the quantity, not automatically its final value.

If the starting quantity is 5, the final quantity is 14. A learner who answers 9 for both prompts has missed the role of the initial value. More practice finding antiderivatives alone might not repair that interpretation.

A useful tutor connects the expression with an area on a graph and the language of accumulation. Then the learner should interpret a changed example without being told which concept to use.

## Look for careful handling of conditions

Ask how a tutor teaches when a conclusion is justified. Knowing the name of a theorem is not enough if the student cannot check its hypotheses in the problem provided.

Similarly, a derivative equal to zero identifies a stationary candidate but does not by itself establish a maximum. The learner needs relevant evidence about the function's behaviour. Teaching should distinguish a promising calculation from a complete argument.

Ask for an explanation that the student can follow. Accurate ordinary language can support learning before a concise mathematical justification is written. The lesson should not become a recital of terminology the learner cannot apply.

## Check course and examination fit

Use College Board's [AP Calculus AB assessment guide](https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-calculus-ab/assessment) for the intended exam. Its current information includes the May 2027 arrangements. A tutor should distinguish those details from the format of older practice material.

Preparation also needs both calculator and non-calculator reasoning as appropriate. Ask how the learner practises selecting a method and interpreting a technological output, rather than relying on calculator entry as a substitute for understanding.

Our article on [Calculus FRQs beyond differentiation](ap-calculus-frq-beyond-differentiation.html) explores why a correct technique may still be an incomplete response. A tutor should be able to give feedback on the missing explanation.

## Find out how the student works online

Ask how written attempts are shared during the lesson. The teacher needs to see signs, notation and changes of direction, not just hear a final number. A shared screen can support that work while paper or another suitable method makes the student's reasoning visible.

After a model, the learner should attempt a fresh task. For the rate example, change the interval or starting quantity and ask for an interpretation. A familiar answer repeated immediately is less informative than a later independent solution.

Practice between sessions should be manageable and connected with the teaching. Ask how it is reviewed and what happens if the original misconception returns.

Bring the course, assessment year, current topic and a recent response to your consultation. Mention whether the concern is calculus itself, earlier algebra, written justification or handling unfamiliar representations.
