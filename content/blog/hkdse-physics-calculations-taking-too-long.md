Slow **HKDSE Physics calculations** are rarely caused by the calculator alone. The delay usually begins earlier: choosing a principle, organising the data, converting units or deciding which step comes first.

Speed improves when the method becomes consistent.

## Find where the time is going

Watch the student complete several questions and identify the bottleneck.

Are they:

- searching randomly through equations?
- rereading the question many times?
- hesitating over unit conversions?
- struggling to rearrange formulas?
- typing into the calculator before planning?
- restarting because the working is unclear?

Timing a full paper will show that the student is slow. Timing individual stages shows why.

## Use a fixed calculation routine

For every numerical problem:

1. State the relevant physical relationship.
2. List or mark the known quantities.
3. Convert units before substitution.
4. Rearrange clearly where necessary.
5. Substitute values with units.
6. Check whether the answer is sensible.

This may initially look slower than jumping straight to the calculator, but it prevents costly restarts and becomes faster with repetition.

## Practise algebra and units outside full questions

If formula rearrangement is weak, a student wastes Physics practice fighting algebra. Use short drills involving powers of ten, prefixes, standard form, significant figures and rearrangement.

Then return those skills to physical contexts. The aim is not isolated mathematics forever; it is removing friction from the Physics.

## Build speed in small sets

Do not begin with repeated full papers. Choose four or five questions using the same relationship, then progress to mixed questions where the student must select the relationship independently.

Record accuracy first. Once the method is stable, set a reasonable time limit and reduce it gradually.

## Know when a calculation is actually a concept problem

A student may manipulate equations quickly but still pause because they do not understand the situation. In that case, more speed drills will not help.
