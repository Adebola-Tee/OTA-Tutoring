Searching for an **online English tutor in Japan** can lead to very different lessons: everyday conversation, school reading, essay writing or examination preparation. For a child attending an international school, the right starting point is the task they need to complete in class.

A learner may chat comfortably in English yet struggle to explain an idea in a paragraph. Another may write accurately but hesitate to speak in a group. Neither situation tells us that all their English skills are weak. We need a clearer picture before choosing the lesson plan.

## Match the course and the task

Ask the school which curriculum and English course the child follows. British, American and IB programmes do not use identical terminology or assessment tasks. Bring the assignment instructions, a sample of the child's work and the teacher's feedback.

For an IB MYP learner, check whether support concerns language acquisition or language and literature. The [IB's language acquisition guidance](https://ibo.org/programmes/middle-years-programme/curriculum/language-acquisition/) describes phases that do not simply correspond to age or school year. A generic lesson for a twelve year old may therefore miss the learner's actual language needs.

Agree one outcome for the first few sessions. It might be explaining a reading inference with evidence, contributing a reason in discussion or organising a paragraph. A specific task makes it easier to recognise progress.

## Use speaking to prepare thinking for writing

Begin with a question about a short text or familiar topic. Ask the learner to give an answer and a reason orally. If the idea is unclear, discuss it before asking for a polished sentence.

Then record a few useful words, not a complete answer for the learner to copy. Ask them to turn their spoken explanation into two or three connected sentences. Compare the written version with what they meant to say.

This helps distinguish an ideas problem from a language problem. A learner who has nothing specific to say needs support developing the thought. One who explains it well aloud but loses it on paper may need sentence organisation, vocabulary or a writing routine.

## Teach vocabulary inside meaningful examples

Collect a small number of words from the current school topic. Check meaning, pronunciation and how the word behaves in a sentence. Ask for an example that fits the topic rather than a definition learned by itself.

For example, a learner using compare should understand that the response needs a relationship between things. Practice can begin with familiar objects before moving to two characters or arguments in a text.

Return to the words in a fresh task later. Recognition during the lesson is only a starting point. The useful question is whether the child can choose and use the language independently when they need it.

## Make feedback specific enough to use

Correct the issue that most affects the current task. If a paragraph has no clear main point, fixing every spelling error first may leave the bigger difficulty unchanged. If the ideas are organised but sentences repeatedly confuse the meaning, work on that pattern.

Show one example, explain the correction and let the learner revise another sentence independently. Keep their original attempt so the improvement is visible. A tutor's rewritten paragraph may be fluent without teaching the learner how to produce it.

End with a short new response. That is a better check than asking whether the learner understood. At the next lesson, review what transferred and what still needed a prompt.

## Choose a routine that fits school life in Japan

Confirm the lesson time in Japan Standard Time and agree how any timetable changes will be communicated. The learning plan should fit school assignments, activities and rest. Adding a separate pile of English work can make support harder to maintain.

Ask how lesson notes and practice will reach the parent, and how much adult help is expected. Families should know whether the next task is speaking practice, reading or independent writing rather than receiving a vague instruction to practise English more.
