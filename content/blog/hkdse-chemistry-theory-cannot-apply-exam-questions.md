Some students can define key terms, describe familiar reactions and repeat notes accurately, yet struggle when an **HKDSE Chemistry exam question** presents a new substance, experiment or set of data.

They know the theory, but they have not yet learned to recognise where and how to use it.

## Application begins with translation

An unfamiliar context often contains familiar Chemistry underneath. The student must translate the information into particles, bonding, energetics, equilibrium, redox, acids and bases, or another relevant principle.

Before answering, ask:

1. What observation or change must I explain?
2. Which topic is hidden inside the question?
3. What evidence has the question provided?
4. How does the theory connect to that evidence?

## Memorised phrases are not enough

Students sometimes insert correct keywords without building a logical chain. An effective explanation connects cause and effect in the specific context.

Instead of writing a general rule, the student should name the relevant particles or quantities, state what changes and explain why that produces the observed result.

## Practise variations of one concept

Do not jump immediately between unrelated past-paper questions. Choose one concept and practise it across several contexts.

For example, equilibrium can be tested through concentration, pressure, temperature, industrial conditions and interpretation of data. Comparing those versions teaches students to recognise the same principle beneath different wording.

## Use the mark scheme after thinking

Attempt the question fully before checking the answer. Then identify the missing reasoning, not just the missing words.

Close the mark scheme and rewrite the response. A few days later, test the same skill with a fresh question.

Students should use materials aligned with the official [HKEAA Chemistry assessment framework](https://www.hkeaa.edu.hk/en/hkdse/assessment/subject_information/category_a_subjects/chem/).

## Check whether a hidden content gap exists

Sometimes “application difficulty” is incomplete knowledge. Ask the student to explain the underlying concept without the exam context. If that explanation is weak, repair the knowledge first.
