A Year 8 student can understand a story perfectly well and still struggle to write an answer that earns credit. An **online Year 8 English tutor** should help the student move from “I know what the text says” to a supported interpretation in their own words.

## Find out what kind of answer goes wrong

Ask your child to show a recent task and the teacher's comments. Did they retell the plot rather than answer the question? Use a quotation with no explanation? Have ideas but write only one sentence? Or misunderstand the language in the extract? Each calls for a different lesson.

Take this simple example: a character stands by a locked door and keeps checking the corridor. A student says, “He is nervous.” The next question is *why do you think so?* Pointing to the repeated checking begins an evidence-based answer. Explaining what that action suggests takes it further.

## Teach a repeatable paragraph routine

One practical routine is:

1. Answer the exact question in a sentence.
2. Select a short, relevant detail from the text.
3. Explain how that detail supports the idea.
4. Ask whether another reading is possible.

Do not turn the routine into a rigid template that produces identical paragraphs. The student needs to understand the thinking behind it. Start with a short passage and gradually reduce prompts.

## What about creative writing?

The same child may need help writing stories or descriptions. Ask them to plan one scene, write it, and revise for clarity and effect. Reading a sentence aloud can expose an awkward sequence or missing detail. Feedback should focus on a few choices the student can change themselves.

An online tutor can use a shared document to show how one revision improves a paragraph, then return control of the keyboard to the learner. ## A ten-minute practice task

Choose a paragraph from the student's current book. Ask them to make one claim about a character, underline a supporting detail and explain how that detail supports the claim. Then challenge them: could another reader interpret the detail differently?

Keep the task small. A strong single paragraph written independently is more informative than three pages copied from a model. Save the first attempt and repeat the exercise with a different passage two weeks later. The comparison can show whether the student has begun to select better evidence and explain it with less prompting.
