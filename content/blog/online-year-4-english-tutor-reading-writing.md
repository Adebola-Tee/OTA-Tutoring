When a parent searches for an online Year 4 English tutor, they may want help with reading, writing or both. A child who enjoys stories might struggle to explain a character's feelings. Another may tell an imaginative story aloud but find it hard to organise the same ideas on paper. The first lesson should find that difference.

For schools following England's [national curriculum for English](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-english-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-english-programmes-of-study), Years 3 and 4 share a programme of study. Reading discussions, evidence, vocabulary and the process of planning and improving writing all matter. Tuition should connect these skills to your child's current school work.

## Listen to the reading before choosing worksheets

Ask the child to read a short, suitable passage aloud. Notice whether they decode accurately, pause meaningfully and understand unfamiliar words. If they use most of their effort working out the words, comprehension questions may need a different starting point from those for a fluent reader.

Next, ask them to summarise what happened in one or two sentences. This checks whether they have followed the passage as a whole. A pupil may remember a striking detail but miss the main event or confuse which character did something.

## Make answers depend on clues

Try this original sentence: “Noah pushed his drawing to the edge of the desk and looked away when the teacher approached.” Ask how Noah might feel about showing the drawing. His actions could suggest uncertainty or embarrassment, but the text does not prove that the drawing is bad.

Have the child point to the clue and explain why it supports their answer. Then change the sentence so Noah holds up the drawing and calls the teacher over. Discuss how the new actions change the interpretation. This teaches careful inference without asking the child to memorise a stock answer.

## Turn an interesting idea into a clear sentence

If your child says, “The dragon was scary and everyone ran,” ask what made the dragon frightening. They might add, “The dragon's claws scraped the gate, and the villagers rushed indoors.” The new sentence gives the reader a visible action and a response.

Do not replace the child's whole paragraph with an adult version. Choose one improvement they understand, let them revise it and ask them to use the same idea in a new sentence. Useful feedback leaves the writer with a decision they can make again independently.

## Teach grammar through meaning

Grammar practice works better when the child sees its purpose. Compare “After dinner we played outside” with “We played outside after dinner.” Ask what changes when the time phrase moves. Discuss the comma in “After dinner, we played outside” and connect the punctuation to a sentence they are writing.

Similarly, compare pronouns in a paragraph. If two characters could both be “he,” the writer may need to use a name to make the meaning clear. Editing should include checking what the reader understands, not only correcting spelling in isolation.

## Look for an active online lesson

Your child should spend time reading, talking, making choices and writing. A shared screen can show a passage or a model, but the tutor needs to hear the child's reasoning and see their own attempt. Short tasks can work well when they have a clear purpose and useful feedback.

Ask what practice will follow the lesson and how much help you should give. Ten focused minutes with a specific task can be more informative than a long worksheet completed with constant prompting. ## Check progress through independent work

Keep an early reading response and a short writing sample. After several lessons, use a fresh passage or prompt of similar difficulty. Compare whether answers use better evidence, sentences communicate more clearly and the child needs fewer reminders.

School feedback remains useful, but progress should also be visible in the work. Ask the tutor to explain the next priority rather than simply describe every lesson as successful.
