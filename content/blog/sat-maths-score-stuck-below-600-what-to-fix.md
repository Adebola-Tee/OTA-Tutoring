You study.

You take another SAT practice test.

And there it is again:

**550. 570. 590.**

If your **SAT Maths score keeps getting stuck below 600**, doing more full practice tests is not necessarily the answer.

At this point, you need to find out **what keeps taking the marks away**.

Because a student stuck at 580 for three tests usually does not have a “Maths problem”.

They have a few specific problems that keep repeating.

## First: stop looking only at the total score

A 580 tells you where you finished.

It does not tell you why.

SAT Maths currently tests four main areas:

* Algebra
* Advanced Math
* Problem-Solving and Data Analysis
* Geometry and Trigonometry

According to College Board, Algebra and Advanced Math make up the largest share of the section, with roughly **13–15 questions from each area**.

So if algebraic manipulation, linear equations, quadratics or nonlinear functions are shaky, that weakness can show up again and again across the test.

Before taking another full practice test, find out **which domain is consistently weakest**.

## 1. Your Algebra may not be as secure as you think

SAT Algebra is not simply:

**“Can you solve for x?”**

You may need to recognise linear relationships, work with systems of equations, interpret functions or build an equation from a word problem.

A student can understand basic Algebra in school and still struggle when the SAT presents the same idea differently.

If you frequently find yourself thinking:

**“I knew this topic, but I didn't know what the question wanted,”**

you probably need more SAT-style application practice — not another Algebra lesson from the beginning.

## 2. Advanced Math may be quietly destroying your score

This is a common one.

Quadratics.

Exponentials.

Equivalent expressions.

Polynomials.

Radicals.

Nonlinear equations.

Students often practise easier Algebra because it feels productive while repeatedly avoiding the questions they actually find difficult.

That creates a ceiling.

Go through your recent practice tests and count how many missed questions came from **Advanced Math**.

The pattern may be much clearer than you expect.

## 3. Word problems are slowing you down

College Board says around **30% of SAT Maths questions are set in context**.

So knowing how to calculate is not enough.

You also have to translate words into Maths.

When reading a question, ask:

**What do I know?**

**What am I trying to find?**

**What relationship connects them?**

If you immediately start calculating because you recognise some numbers, you may be solving the wrong problem perfectly.

## 4. You know the Maths — but make too many small mistakes

Sometimes the gap between your current score and 600 is not another difficult topic.

It is:

* a negative sign
* entering something incorrectly
* misreading a percentage
* copying the wrong number
* answering for x when the question asked for something else
* rushing an easy question

Look at your last practice test.

How many missed questions did you genuinely **not know how to solve**?

Now compare that with the questions where you looked at the answer afterwards and said:

**“I knew that.”**

Those careless marks matter.

## 5. You are not using Desmos strategically

The digital SAT includes access to the built-in Desmos graphing calculator throughout the Maths section.

But there are two extremes.

Some students barely use it.

Others try to use it for everything.

Neither is ideal.

Desmos can be extremely useful for graphing equations, finding intersections, checking solutions and handling certain function questions quickly.

But basic algebraic fluency still matters.

The goal is to know:

**when Desmos saves time — and when doing the Maths yourself is faster.**

## 6. Timing may be the real problem

The SAT Maths section currently contains **44 questions across two 35-minute modules**.

That gives you 70 minutes in total.

If the final few questions in each module are constantly rushed or guessed, your score problem may partly be pacing.

Do not spend three minutes proving to yourself that one difficult question can be solved while easier marks are still waiting.

Practise recognising when to move on.

## 7. You keep taking tests without fixing what the tests reveal

This may be the biggest issue.

Practice Test 1: 570.

Practice Test 2: 580.

Practice Test 3: 570.

Practice Test 4: 590.

At some point, another test is not preparation.

It is simply **measuring the same weaknesses again**.

After each practice test, create a mistake list:

**Topic | Why I got it wrong | What I need to fix**

Then spend your next study sessions fixing those errors before taking another full test.


## What should you work on first to get above 600?

Do not start with the hardest SAT Maths questions you can find.

Start with the questions you are **capable of getting right but currently losing**.

That usually means:

1. Fix repeated Algebra and Advanced Math weaknesses.
2. Reduce careless errors.
3. Improve word-problem translation.
4. Learn when Desmos is useful.
5. Practise under timed conditions.
6. Review every mistake before taking another full test.

That is a much stronger strategy than simply saying:

**“I need to practise more Maths.”**
