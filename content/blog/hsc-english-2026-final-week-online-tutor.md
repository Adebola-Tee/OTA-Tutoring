The [NSW Education Standards Authority announcement](https://www.nsw.gov.au/education-and-training/nesa/news/all/2026-hsc-timetables-released) places the start of the 2026 written HSC exams, with English Paper 1, on Tuesday 13 October. For students preparing now, the final week is an opportunity to make existing knowledge more usable under exam conditions.

That does not mean writing a new model essay every night. A learner may know their text well but struggle to shape that knowledge around an unfamiliar question. Focused English tutoring is most useful when it reveals that gap and helps the student practise a response independently.

## Start with your actual course and teacher guidance

Check the student's English course, prescribed text, paper requirements and individual timetable. English Standard, Advanced and other courses should not be treated as interchangeable. Use the relevant official materials and school advice when selecting practice questions.

Bring a recent marked response to the review. Identify whether the problem is answering the question, selecting evidence, explaining its significance or completing the task. A broad instruction to “add analysis” will not help much unless the student can see what analysis needs to do.

Choose one improvement that can appear in the next answer. For instance, every paragraph might need a clearer connection between its main claim and the question's key idea. This is easier to practise than trying to improve everything simultaneously.

## Practise changing an argument when the question changes

Use this original teaching prompt: “How does a text show that belonging can change a person's choices?” First identify the relationship being tested. The response needs to connect belonging with decisions, rather than simply list moments when a character feels accepted.

A possible starting claim is that the desire for acceptance makes a character compromise their beliefs. The student must then choose evidence from their own text and explain how it supports that claim. A technique label helps only if its use contributes to the argument.

Now change the prompt to ask about resistance to belonging. Which evidence still works? Which claim needs changing? This exercise exposes the limits of a memorised essay and encourages flexible planning. These are our example prompts, not predictions of the examination.

## Make a small evidence bank earn its place

Review a manageable selection of evidence with several possible uses. For each example, note its context, the idea it supports and how the language or form develops that idea. A quotation remembered without its meaning is difficult to adapt.

Test the evidence aloud. Ask the learner to explain it in ordinary language before writing a paragraph. If the explanation becomes vague, return to the passage rather than adding impressive vocabulary around an unclear thought.

Then write one paragraph and revise it. Remove plot details that do not help answer the question. Check that the explanation develops the claim and that the final sentence links back to the task without merely repeating its words.

## Use timed practice to find a practical adjustment

Attempt a fresh section with the appropriate time allocation for the course. Record how long planning took and where the answer slowed down. Review whether an overlong introduction or a difficult early question crowded out later work.

For unseen material, practise reading the exact demand, selecting relevant details and matching the response length to the task. Avoid spending a long time naming every feature you can see. The answer needs to explain the features that matter to the question.

Finish the session by setting one adjustment for the next attempt. It might be a shorter plan, quicker evidence selection or a more concise opening. Keep enough space between demanding tasks to review them properly.

## Ask for feedback on your own writing

An online HSC English tutor should work with your course and help you improve your response, while keeping authorship with the student. Ask what feedback you will receive and what you should practise afterwards. One reviewed answer can be more useful than several unread essays.
