“A-level Chemistry is hard” is true for many students, but it does not tell an **online A-level Chemistry tutor** where to start. The course combines physical, inorganic and organic ideas. A calculation problem, a reaction pathway and an explanation question can go wrong for completely different reasons.

## If calculations are the problem

Begin with a worked question *you attempted*. List what you know, the units you have and the quantity you need. For a mole problem, do you know which substances react, the relevant ratio and how to convert the final result into the requested unit? If a student inserts numbers into a formula before checking those steps, a small change in wording may throw them off.

A tutor should model a method, then ask you to explain why each step works. Follow it with a new question that uses the same principle in a different context.

## If organic chemistry is the problem

Do not start by memorising a long wall of reactions. Draw the starting material and product. Identify which bond changes, what conditions are given and why a particular route makes sense. Ask which distinctions you need for *your* syllabus. Mechanisms become easier to recall when you can explain electron movement rather than reproduce arrows mechanically.

The [AQA A-level Chemistry subject content](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/chemistry/a-level/chemistry-7405/specification/subject-content) separates physical, inorganic and organic Chemistry. A tutor should still check your exact exam board and current teaching sequence before planning lessons.

## How should lessons be organised?

Take a recent assessment and tag errors by cause: concept, calculation, reaction knowledge, interpretation of data or written explanation. Give priority to recurring mistakes. Practise a few mixed questions so you learn to choose a method without being told the topic in advance.

Finish each lesson with a short independent task and review it next time. A student who can explain one mechanism to the tutor but cannot recognise it on a new paper needs more varied retrieval and application.

## Before booking

Ask which A-level specifications the tutor teaches, what recent work they want to see and how they will track whether a weak topic has improved. ## Try a quick self-check before the first lesson

Take one missed calculation and write a sentence next to each line of working explaining what that line represents. If you cannot explain a conversion, that is probably where the teaching should begin. For an organic question, circle the functional group in the starting material and in the product. Identify the structural change before recalling reagents and conditions.

Keep the original incorrect answer. A tutor who sees the whole attempt can identify a repeated thinking pattern. An answer key with only the final result cannot show whether the student misunderstood the chemistry or made one arithmetic slip.
