For many IGCSE students and parents in Kuwait, results day in 2026 comes with more questions than usual.

Students spent months preparing for the June examination series, only for the exam arrangements in Kuwait to change. Now, as results day approaches, families are not only wondering what grades they will receive. They also want to understand how those grades were calculated, whether they will be accepted normally, and what to do if a result is lower than expected.

Cambridge IGCSE and O Level results for the June 2026 series are due on **18 August 2026**, while Pearson Edexcel International GCSE results will be released to students on **20 August 2026**.

If you are waiting for IGCSE results in Kuwait, here is what you need to know.

## Why IGCSE results in Kuwait are different in 2026

The June 2026 Cambridge examinations did not go ahead as originally planned in Kuwait.

Cambridge International cancelled the June examination series in Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar and the UAE. Instead, affected students entered for Cambridge IGCSE, O Level, AS and A Level qualifications are receiving grades through the **Portfolio of Evidence** route.

Pearson Edexcel also introduced contingency arrangements for students affected by the cancellation of examinations in Kuwait.

This means some students receiving IGCSE results this August did not complete the usual written examination series they originally prepared for.

Naturally, one of the biggest questions from parents is simple.

**How was my child's grade actually decided?**

## How Cambridge Portfolio of Evidence grades work

Cambridge's Portfolio of Evidence route allows students to receive a qualification when examinations cannot proceed normally.

Instead of relying on the cancelled written papers, schools submitted authenticated examples of students' work that demonstrated their knowledge and ability across the course.

Cambridge examiners then assessed that evidence using Cambridge's normal standards.

Cambridge has also stated that grades awarded through the Portfolio of Evidence route should be recognised as equivalent to grades achieved through external examinations.

That is important for families worried that a portfolio grade somehow makes the qualification less valuable.

It does not.

Students following the Portfolio of Evidence route receive their results alongside other Cambridge candidates.

For Cambridge IGCSE and O Level students, results will be released on **18 August 2026**.

## What about Pearson Edexcel IGCSE students in Kuwait?

Pearson's contingency arrangements are slightly different.

Depending on the student's qualification and the assessment evidence available, grades may be determined using existing assessment information or through Pearson's International Contingency Grading process supported by a portfolio of evidence.

Pearson Edexcel International GCSE results for the June 2026 series will be released to students on **20 August 2026**.

If you are unsure which grading route was used for your child, speak directly with the school or examination centre. They will be able to explain how the final result was determined.

## What should you do if your IGCSE result is lower than expected?

First, do not panic.

A disappointing result can feel enormous on results day, especially when a student believes they were capable of achieving more.

But before immediately requesting an appeal or deciding on a retake, look at the bigger picture.

Consider:

- how close the grade is to what the student expected
- whether the grade affects the student's next course or school plans
- previous school assessment results
- whether there are obvious gaps in the student's understanding
- whether the student genuinely needs a higher grade

The next step should usually be a conversation with the school or examination centre.

Cambridge's **Enquiries About Results** service is available to students whose grades were awarded through the Portfolio of Evidence route.

Pearson also provides post-results services for International GCSE students.

For the Summer 2026 International GCSE series, Pearson's review services open on **20 August 2026**, with key review deadlines running until **24 September 2026**.

It is also important to understand that requesting a review does not automatically mean a grade will increase.

## Should your child appeal or retake an IGCSE?

This depends on why the result was disappointing.

A student who narrowly missed an important grade may want to speak with their school about the appropriate review process.

A student who has substantial gaps in Maths, English, Biology, Chemistry or Physics may need something different.

In that case, simply chasing a different grade may not solve the real problem.

For example, a student may technically pass IGCSE Maths while still struggling with algebra, fractions or multi-step problem solving. Another student may achieve a reasonable Biology grade but still have weak understanding of important topics that will return at a higher level.

Moving forward without addressing those weaknesses can make the next stage much harder.

Sometimes the more useful question is not:

**“Can we get this grade changed?”**

It is:

**“What does my child need to improve before the next stage?”**

## Preparing for an IGCSE retake in Kuwait

If a retake becomes necessary, preparation should not simply mean repeating the same study routine.

Start by identifying what went wrong.

Was the student struggling with subject knowledge?

Was time management the problem?

Did they understand the content but lose marks because of exam technique?

Were they relying too heavily on memorisation?

A strong retake plan should identify:

- topics the student already understands well
- areas where marks are repeatedly lost
- gaps in foundational knowledge
- weaknesses in exam technique
- realistic weekly study targets
- when past papers should be introduced

Students usually make better progress when preparation is targeted rather than trying to revise the entire syllabus again from the beginning.

## What students in Kuwait should do after results day

Results day should be treated as information, not a verdict on a student's ability.

For some students, the next step will be celebrating and moving forward.

For others, it may mean reviewing a result, preparing for another examination series or getting additional help in a subject that has become difficult.

Parents should use the weeks after results day to understand exactly where support is needed.

## Kuwait IGCSE results 2026 frequently asked questions

### When are Cambridge IGCSE results released in Kuwait?

Cambridge IGCSE and O Level results for the June 2026 series will be released on **18 August 2026**.

### When are Edexcel International GCSE results released in Kuwait?

Pearson Edexcel International GCSE results will be released to students on **20 August 2026**.

### Were Cambridge IGCSE exams cancelled in Kuwait in 2026?

Yes. Cambridge cancelled the June 2026 examination series in Kuwait and used its Portfolio of Evidence route for affected Cambridge IGCSE, O Level, AS and A Level students.

### What is a Portfolio of Evidence grade?

A Portfolio of Evidence grade is awarded using authenticated examples of a student's work rather than the cancelled external examinations. Cambridge examiners assess the evidence according to Cambridge's normal standards.

### Are Portfolio of Evidence grades recognised by universities?

Cambridge states that these grades represent the same standards and level of attainment as externally examined grades and has asked universities and other recognising organisations to treat them as equivalent.

### Can Cambridge IGCSE students request a review?

Yes. Cambridge has confirmed that its Enquiries About Results service is available to candidates who followed the Portfolio of Evidence route.

### Can Edexcel International GCSE results be reviewed?

Yes. Pearson offers post-results review services for International GCSE qualifications. Students and parents should normally speak with their school or examination centre first.

### Should my child retake an IGCSE?

A retake may make sense if the grade affects the student's next academic step or if the student needs a stronger foundation before progressing. The decision should be based on the student's goals and the reason behind the original result, not simply disappointment on results day.
