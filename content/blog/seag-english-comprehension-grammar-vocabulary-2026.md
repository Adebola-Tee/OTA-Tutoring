If your child is preparing for the **SEAG Transfer Test 2026**, English revision should now be much more targeted than simply “doing more reading”.

The official SEAG English specification covers four main areas:

* **Comprehension**
* **Spelling and Punctuation**
* **Grammar and Syntax**
* **Vocabulary**

The assessment is based on the Northern Ireland Key Stage 2 curriculum.

The key question for parents is therefore not:

**“Is my child good at English?”**

It is:

**“Which type of English question is costing my child marks?”**

## 1. Comprehension

Comprehension is one of the most important areas to practise.

Children may need to:

* retrieve information directly from a passage
* make inferences
* understand why something happened
* identify meaning from context
* distinguish between similar answer choices

One of the biggest mistakes pupils make is answering from memory instead of returning to the text.

Teach your child to ask:

**Where is the evidence for my answer?**

If they cannot point to something in the passage that supports their choice, they should reconsider it.

## 2. Vocabulary

Vocabulary questions are not simply about memorising long lists of difficult words.

Children need to understand what a word means **within the sentence or passage where it appears**.

When your child meets an unfamiliar word, encourage them to:

1. read the sentence again
2. look at the words around it
3. think about the overall meaning
4. replace the unfamiliar word with each possible answer

This develops vocabulary much more effectively than memorising definitions alone.

## 3. Grammar and Syntax

Your child should be comfortable recognising whether sentences are written correctly.

Revision may include:

* nouns, verbs and adjectives
* pronouns
* verb agreement
* tenses
* sentence structure
* word order
* choosing grammatically correct sentences

Grammar becomes easier when children understand **why** an answer is correct rather than relying only on what “sounds right”.

## 4. Spelling and Punctuation

Small errors matter.

Children should revise familiar spelling patterns and become confident using punctuation correctly, including:

* capital letters
* full stops
* commas
* apostrophes
* question marks
* speech punctuation where appropriate

When practising, ask your child to explain what is wrong with an incorrect sentence rather than simply choosing the correct version.

That strengthens the rule behind the answer.

## Do not practise each skill only in isolation

A child might perform brilliantly on a worksheet labelled **Grammar**, but struggle when grammar appears unexpectedly inside a mixed SEAG paper.

That is why preparation should gradually move from individual topics towards **mixed English practice**.

One session might include:

* a short comprehension passage
* vocabulary questions
* grammar
* spelling and punctuation

This better reflects the thinking pupils need during the real assessment.

## Watch for careless reading

Sometimes the problem is not English knowledge at all.

It is rushing.

Common mistakes include:

* missing words such as **not**
* choosing the first answer that sounds reasonable
* failing to read all options
* overlooking a clue in the passage
* changing a correct answer unnecessarily

If your child frequently says, **“Oh, I knew that,”** after seeing the answer, the issue may be exam technique rather than knowledge.

## Use official SEAG practice materials

Each SEAG assessment paper contains **28 English questions and 28 Maths questions**, with 60 minutes for the main test.

SEAG also provides official practice papers, answer sheets and a Parents’ Guide with answer keys free on its website.

Use these papers to identify patterns.

If most mistakes are comprehension questions, spend more time there.

If vocabulary is consistently weak, build vocabulary-in-context practice into the week.

If your child knows the answers but runs out of time, begin introducing timed sets.

## What should SEAG English revision look like now?

A simple weekly routine could include:

**One comprehension session**

Focus on evidence, inference and careful reading.

**One grammar and vocabulary session**

Target weaker rules and unfamiliar words.

**One mixed English session**

Combine different question types.

**One timed practice session**

Build speed without sacrificing accuracy.

The aim is not to overwhelm your child.

It is to make the same mistakes appear **less and less often**.

## There is still time to improve before November

SEAG Paper 1 takes place on **Saturday 14 November 2026**, followed by Paper 2 on **Saturday 21 November 2026**.

A few focused weeks can make a significant difference, particularly when revision is based on the exact areas where marks are currently being lost.
