A child who asks “why?” all the time may be ready for more Science, even when school reports look fine. An **online Year 5 Science tutor** can help turn that curiosity into careful observation, simple tests and clear explanations. Tutoring can also rebuild confidence when new vocabulary makes a familiar idea feel complicated.

## What can an online Science lesson actually do?

Start with a question the learner cares about: why does one material dissolve in water while another does not? Ask the child to predict, describe a fair comparison and say what they would look for. With an appropriate adult present for any physical activity, a simple household observation can become a discussion about evidence. A tutor can also use safe images, videos or a data table instead of asking a child to carry out an experiment.

The [primary Science curriculum in England](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/national-curriculum-in-england-science-programmes-of-study/national-curriculum-in-england-science-programmes-of-study) includes scientific enquiry alongside subject knowledge. If your child's school uses another curriculum, share its current unit.

## Help a child explain what they noticed

An answer such as “it disappeared” is an observation, not a complete explanation. Ask, “What did you see?”, “What changed?”, and “What do you think happened to the material?” Encourage the child to distinguish what the test showed from what they are guessing.

For a plant-growth example, students can compare two sets of observations. Did both plants receive the same amount of water? If several conditions changed at once, can the test really show which one mattered? A small, age-appropriate discussion develops reasoning that becomes valuable later in secondary Science.

## The role of the tutor

The learner should make predictions, describe evidence and try again after feedback. Watching a long slideshow about scientific facts does little to reveal what they think. The tutor can introduce the correct terms once the child has had a chance to express the idea in their own words.

Ask what the child will be able to *do* after a few lessons: explain a simple process, read a table, or plan a fair comparison. That is clearer than a promise of “advanced Science.”

## A short reasoning activity for home

Show your child two pictures of the same plant taken a week apart. Ask what they can *observe* in each picture and what they would need to know before explaining a difference. Was the amount of water the same? Was one plant placed in less light? Do they have enough information to decide?

The child does not need a sophisticated scientific report. One observation, one possible explanation and one question to investigate are enough. This helps a tutor see whether the learner can separate evidence from an assumption, a skill that supports later Biology, Chemistry and Physics.
