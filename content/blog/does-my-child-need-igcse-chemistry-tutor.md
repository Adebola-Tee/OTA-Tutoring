One difficult IGCSE Chemistry test does not automatically mean your child needs a tutor. Support becomes more valuable when weaknesses repeat and independent revision is not changing the result.

## Signs that tutoring may help

- new topics keep exposing earlier gaps
- equations and calculations are regularly avoided
- the student understands notes but cannot apply them
- the same corrections return in later tests
- past-paper scores remain flat
- revision has become mostly reading or copying
- confidence and motivation are falling

The most important sign is repeated effort without a clear route to improvement.

## What should be diagnosed first?

A tutor should inspect marked work and distinguish between content, calculations, application, practical skills and exam timing.

Generic syllabus coverage is not enough when the student needs a precise intervention. Cambridge preparation should reflect the official [IGCSE Chemistry 0620 assessment resources](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620/past-papers/); Edexcel students need Pearson-aligned teaching.

## What effective tutoring should look like

Lessons should contain concise explanation, diagnostic questioning, guided practice, independent questions and detailed correction. The student should gradually require fewer prompts and solve unfamiliar questions more confidently.

Progress should be visible in fresh work—not only in how clearly the tutor explained the topic.

## When ongoing tutoring may not be necessary

If the problem is limited to one topic and a short school intervention is already improving results, independent practice may be sufficient. Set a specific target and review the evidence after two or three weeks.
