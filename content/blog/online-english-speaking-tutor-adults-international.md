Adults often search for an **online English speaking tutor** after noticing a specific moment: they understand a meeting but cannot enter the conversation, know the words but lose them under pressure, or avoid a phone call because pronunciation feels uncertain. A lesson plan should begin with that moment, not with a generic promise to “become fluent.”

You do not need to speak perfectly before starting. You do need a clear reason for practising and a tutor who lets you speak enough to notice what happens in real time.

## Choose one situation to practise

Tell the tutor where English matters: a work update, an interview, a conversation with a teacher, travel or everyday social talk. The vocabulary and feedback change with the situation. A learner preparing to explain a technical problem at work needs different practice from one who wants to tell stories more naturally.

A useful first session can include a short conversation, a role-play and a few minutes reviewing the recording or notes. We might notice that the learner pauses because they are searching for complex vocabulary when a simple sentence would work, or because they are unsure how to connect two ideas.

The [British Council's speaking resources](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/skills/speaking) organise practice by level and situation. They are useful for independent practice, but personalised feedback can help you notice your own recurring habits.

## Pronunciation without chasing an accent

The goal is clear communication, not sounding like someone else. Choose sounds or word stress patterns that regularly cause misunderstanding. Practise a word in a short phrase, then in the kind of sentence you actually need to say.

If a learner struggles with a particular consonant, repeating an isolated sound twenty times may not transfer to conversation. We can compare two words, practise a useful sentence and return to that sound during a real exchange. Feedback should be specific enough to use next time.

## Build a repeatable speaking routine

An online lesson might start with a five-minute update, move to a focused role-play and finish with one correction target. Between lessons, record a one-minute answer to a familiar prompt. Listen for the target, make one change and record again.

Progress may be visible as fewer long pauses, a clearer explanation or more willingness to ask a follow-up question. Keep an early recording and compare it with a new, similar task after several weeks. Do not judge progress only by whether a conversation felt comfortable on a single day.

## Questions to ask before booking

- How much of the session will I be speaking?
- Will feedback focus on a few useful changes rather than interrupt every sentence?
- Can we practise the situations I actually face?
- How will we notice improvement in pronunciation and fluency?
