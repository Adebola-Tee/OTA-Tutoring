It can be confusing when a child who was always “good at Science” begins struggling with **IGCSE Chemistry**. The student has not suddenly lost their ability. The subject has changed what it demands from them.

## Knowledge becomes more connected

Earlier Science may reward remembering facts and following familiar procedures. IGCSE Chemistry expects students to connect particle ideas, bonding, equations, energy, rates and quantitative relationships.

A gap in one foundation can appear across several topics.

## Symbolic language becomes essential

Formulae, ionic equations, state symbols and balanced reactions compress chemical ideas into precise notation. A student may understand the explanation verbally but lose marks when translating it into symbols.

## Mathematics carries chemical meaning

Moles, concentration, gas volumes, yield and purity are not ordinary arithmetic exercises. Students must understand the reaction before choosing the calculation.

Our guide to [moles and stoichiometry in IGCSE Chemistry](https://otalearningstudio.com/blog/igcse-chemistry-moles-stoichiometry-students-stuck.html) explains this transition.

## Questions require application

Exams may present unfamiliar substances, experiments or data. The student must recognise a familiar principle inside a new context rather than repeat a memorised paragraph.

## Revision habits must change

Rereading and highlighting can create confidence without retrieval. Strong IGCSE revision combines brief content review, closed-book explanation, focused questions and correction.

Cambridge students should keep preparation aligned with the official [IGCSE Chemistry 0620 resources](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620/past-papers/); Edexcel students should follow their Pearson specification.

## What should parents do next?

Use a recent paper to identify whether the main issue is foundational knowledge, equations, calculations, application or practical questions. Choose two priorities instead of asking the student to revise everything.
