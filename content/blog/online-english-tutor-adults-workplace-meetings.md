Looking for an **online English tutor for adults** often begins with a specific moment at work: you understand a meeting but cannot join in quickly enough, or you prepare an update and lose your words when someone asks a question. General conversation practice can help, but your lessons should also reflect those situations.

Start by describing the task you want to manage more comfortably. Giving a short progress update, explaining a delay and asking for clarification are different demands. A useful first goal is one you can demonstrate, rather than a broad promise to become fluent immediately.

## Build a short update around three ideas

Choose a realistic, non confidential task and explain what has been completed, what is happening now and what needs attention. For example: “I have checked the draft. I am correcting the figures today. I need confirmation of the deadline before I send it.”

The purpose is to make the update easy to follow. You do not need to fill every sentence with formal business vocabulary. First establish the message, then work on grammar and pronunciation that affect its meaning.

A tutor can ask follow up questions about the same task. This moves the lesson beyond reading a prepared script and shows whether you can respond when the conversation changes. Keep private workplace information out of practice materials; an invented scenario can test the same language skills.

## Practise asking for clarification early

A learner may stay silent because asking again feels embarrassing. Prepare a few questions that suit the uncertainty: “Do you mean the draft or the final version?” or “Could you explain what needs changing in the second section?”

These questions are more useful than pretending to understand and discovering the mistake later. Ask the tutor to give instructions with a deliberately unclear detail, then identify the information you need before responding.

Practise listening for the answer as well. Asking a memorised question is only the first half of the exchange. Summarise the agreed action in your own words so both people can check that the instruction was understood.

## Work on disagreement without losing the point

Polite disagreement does not require a long introduction. Try acknowledging the suggestion, stating your concern and offering a practical alternative. For example: “That could work. My concern is the time needed to check the data. Could we send the summary first?”

Discuss how the language might change with a colleague, customer or manager. There is no single sentence that fits every workplace. The aim is to choose wording that respects the relationship while keeping the actual message clear.

Then change the scenario. A tutor should help you adapt the structure, rather than depend on one rehearsed answer. Unexpected questions are useful practice when the task remains manageable and you receive specific feedback afterwards.

## Combine listening, speaking and a brief written recap

Listen to a short exchange and note the decision, responsible person and next action. Retell the main point aloud, then write a short follow up message. This links skills that often occur together in a real meeting.

The [British Council's Business English resources](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/business) include activities for workplace communication, including emails and interview skills. Select material that matches your present level and purpose rather than collecting resources you will never practise.

## Measure clarity rather than accent imitation

Ask whether a listener can understand your update, follow the sequence and identify what you need. A tutor can target unclear word endings, stress or sounds where they affect understanding. The goal is understandable speech, not removing every trace of your existing accent.

With your permission, a short recording can help compare earlier and later attempts. Use the same type of task with new details, so improvement does not depend only on remembering the original script.

## Make the first conversation useful

Bring one situation you avoid, one task you already manage and an example of the language you need next. Discuss a lesson schedule that leaves room for brief practice between sessions.
