## Look for a tutor who starts with the physical situation

Before reaching for an equation, a student should identify what is happening, what is known and what is being asked. A diagram, direction convention or brief model can make the calculation much clearer.

Consider this original example. A vehicle increases its velocity uniformly from 4 m s⁻¹ to 16 m s⁻¹ in 6 seconds. Its acceleration is (16 − 4) ÷ 6 = 2 m s⁻². With uniform acceleration, its average velocity is (4 + 16) ÷ 2 = 10 m s⁻¹, giving a displacement of 60 m.

Ask why uniform acceleration matters. The average of the initial and final velocities is not a method to apply automatically to every changing motion. A tutor should check the model, not just whether the numbers were entered correctly.

## Connect the same example to a graph

On a velocity-time graph for that motion, the line rises from 4 to 16 m s⁻¹ over 6 seconds. Its gradient gives the acceleration. The area beneath the line gives the displacement.

That area consists of a rectangle, 4 × 6 = 24 m, and a triangle, one half × 6 × 12 = 36 m. Together they give 60 m, matching the earlier calculation. Two approaches describe the same motion.

A useful follow-up changes the velocities or includes a section at constant velocity. The student should decide which graph features and equations apply. This checks understanding more effectively than repeating a calculation with identical wording.

## Include explanations and practical reasoning

Physics assessment is not solely numerical. Depending on the unit, the learner may need to interpret measurements, discuss an experiment or explain a relationship. Ask prospective tutors how those tasks appear in lessons and homework.

For practical work, identify the measured variables, how readings are collected and what would make a comparison fair. Distinguish a random variation from a consistent measurement offset. When proposing an improvement, connect it to the actual limitation.

For written explanations, start with the relevant physical principle and connect it to the situation. Adding more sentences without that connection can make an answer longer without making it more accurate. Feedback should show the missing reasoning explicitly.
