IGCSE exam dates depend on the awarding body, subject, paper and examination series. There is no single timetable that every international student can safely follow. Before building a revision calendar, find the course code and exam entry information from your school or centre.

**Checked 1 November 2026:** Pearson has published its final Summer 2027 International GCSE timetable. Cambridge's public timetable page checked for this guide lists June and November 2026 timetables, but does not yet display a June 2027 IGCSE timetable. Ask your school about its latest official information and recheck the awarding body's page.

## Start with the awarding body and examination year

Cambridge IGCSE and Pearson Edexcel International GCSE are different qualifications. A date for GCSE Maths in England is not automatically an International GCSE date. Likewise, International A Level information should not be used to plan an IGCSE entry.

Write down the full qualification, syllabus code, subject route and intended series. Keep your mock dates in a separate part of the calendar because schools arrange those locally. This prevents a mock deadline being mistaken for the external examination date.

## What Pearson confirms for Summer 2027

The [final Pearson Summer 2027 timetable](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/Support/Examination-timetables-for-Edexcel-International-GCSE/int-gcse-summer-2027-final.pdf) lists Mathematics A linear Paper 1F and 1H on **14 May 2027**, with Paper 2F and 2H on **27 May 2027**. Match those codes to your entry before adding them to a personal calendar.

Pearson also publishes separate information for applicable regional R papers. International centres use the relevant start time instructions for their location. Your centre's candidate timetable confirms the local reporting arrangements; a morning label is not a universal clock time across countries.

## How Cambridge timetable checking works

Cambridge allocates centres to administrative zones, with a separate timetable for each zone and a UK timetable. Use the [official Cambridge exam timetables page](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/exam-administration/cambridge-exams-officers-guide/phase-1-preparation/timetabling-exams/exam-timetables/) and confirm the correct version with your exams officer.

Do not substitute the March Checkpoint timetable for a June IGCSE timetable. They refer to different assessments. Until the relevant subject dates are verified, plan around the school calendar and broad preparation milestones without inventing precise examination days.

## November 2026 candidates need a different plan

Students already entered for the current series should check remaining papers immediately. Pearson Mathematics A linear papers are scheduled for 4 and 6 November 2026 in the [final November timetable](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/Support/Examination-timetables-for-Edexcel-International-GCSE/intgcse-nov-2026-final.pdf). Other subjects and routes have different dates.

That is a final preparation situation, not the start of a long revision programme. Confirm entry, equipment and travel arrangements first. Tutoring cannot replace exam registration, and an enquiry about lessons does not secure an examination place.

## Turn the calendar into useful practice

For 2027 preparation, choose one learning priority from recent work rather than distributing equal revision time across every chapter. A learner who cannot interpret ratio needs teaching before repeated timed papers will be useful. A learner who understands methods but leaves questions unfinished needs work on decisions and pacing too.

For example, a recipe uses flour and sugar in the ratio 5 to 2. With 350 g of flour, one part is 70 g and the sugar is 140 g. Change the question to give the total mixture instead, and ask the learner to explain why the calculation changes. This original check exposes whether they understand the role of the whole.

## Review progress before the next assessment

Keep a short record of the mistake, correction and later independent attempt. Build in mixed questions so topics are recognised without headings. A useful timetable has room to revisit learning, not simply move through a list once.
