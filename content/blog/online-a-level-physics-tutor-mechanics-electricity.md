If you are searching for an **online A-level Physics tutor**, “I need help with equations” may hide several different problems. You might not understand the physical situation, you might choose an unsuitable equation, or you might set the problem up correctly and lose the mark in algebra or units.

## Diagnose a mechanics question properly

Take a question about a moving object. Before writing a formula, sketch the system, choose a direction as positive and label the quantities given. Ask yourself whether the acceleration is constant and what quantity the question actually asks for. If you cannot describe the motion, repeating an equation from memory will rarely fix it.

In a one-to-one session, a tutor can watch the moment you choose a method. A student who selects the right approach but rearranges incorrectly needs different practice from someone who cannot draw the force diagram.

## Electricity deserves the same care

For a circuit problem, draw or read the circuit carefully. What is in series and what is in parallel? Which quantities are shared, and which change? Check whether an answer is sensible before accepting it. If your calculated current rises when you add resistance under fixed voltage, revisit the model.

The [AQA A-level Physics specification](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/physics/a-level/physics-7408/specification) includes mechanics and electricity; other exam boards arrange content and assessment differently. Tell the tutor your board and level so the questions match the course.

## How to use a tutoring lesson well

Bring two attempted questions and your working, including any incorrect lines. Ask the tutor to explain the principle behind a method, then attempt a different question without copying the worked solution. Keep a short error record: concept, diagram, equation, algebra, units or explanation.

After the lesson, retry one old question and one new one. If you can only solve the old example, the skill may not have transferred yet. This is more useful evidence than counting the number of pages covered.

## One example of a useful error review

Suppose a student calculated a force but forgot to convert grams to kilograms. Do not file that only under “careless mistake.” Ask whether the unit appeared in the diagram, whether the equation required SI units and whether a rough estimate would have exposed an unreasonable answer.

Now give a different question involving units, without warning that conversion is the focus. If the student catches it independently, the lesson has changed their process. If they miss it again, build a short checklist into the start of every calculation: diagram, quantities, units and intended result.
