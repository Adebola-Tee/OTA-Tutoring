A student may know the definitions, reactions and tests in **Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620** yet still lose marks across an examination paper.

Usually, the knowledge is present but the answer does not use it with enough relevance, precision or evidence.

## Correct knowledge can answer the wrong question

Students often write everything remembered about a topic instead of responding to the command word and context. Before answering, identify whether the question asks them to state, describe, explain, calculate, suggest or predict.

## General language is not chemical explanation

Phrases such as “the particles react more” or “it becomes more stable” may contain the right direction but lack the detail needed for marks.

Strong answers name the relevant particles, bonds, forces, collisions or quantities and connect them through cause and effect.

## Practical questions require reasoning

Memorising a method is not enough when the apparatus or substance changes. Students should understand variables, measurements, sources of error, improvements and how observations support a conclusion.

## Mark schemes must be used actively

After marking, the student should identify the missing idea, close the answer and rewrite their response. Then test the same skill with a fresh question.

Cambridge publishes official [0620 past papers and examiner reports](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620/past-papers/). Examiner reports can reveal recurring issues with precision and interpretation.
