Running out of time on ACT Science does not always mean a student reads too slowly. Often, time disappears through unnecessary reading, repeated checking or uncertainty about where to find evidence.

Telling a child to “go faster” can increase panic without changing the habit causing the delay. Start by locating where the minutes are going.

## The student may be reading every passage in full

ACT Science presents scientific scenarios, but many questions can be answered by moving directly between the prompt and the relevant graph, table or paragraph.

Students should not ignore the passage. They should read with a purpose. Begin with enough orientation to identify the topic, figures and experiments, then let each question direct attention to the evidence needed.

## Graph navigation may be too slow

Before extracting a value, check:

- which figure the question names
- the x- and y-axes
- units and scale
- line, symbol or experimental group
- direction of comparison

This ten-second routine prevents much longer rereading after the wrong value has been chosen.

## The student may be calculating when estimation is enough

Some questions require precise work, but others can be solved by recognising a trend or eliminating impossible options. Writing out elaborate calculations for every numerical item wastes time.

Teach the student to decide whether the task asks for an exact value, a comparison, an interpolation or a prediction.

## Difficult questions are receiving unlimited time

A student can lose several later questions by refusing to leave one confusing item. Agree on a sensible cut-off. Mark the best-supported option, flag the question if the format allows and return only after completing more accessible items.

This is not giving up. It protects the entire section.

## Timing practice may be too large

Full sections show that pacing is weak, but they do not teach a replacement strategy by themselves. Practise one passage at a time while recording:

- orientation time
- time per question
- where rereading occurred
- accuracy
- reason for each delay

Once the new method is stable, combine passages and return to full timed sections.

The official [ACT Science description](https://www.act.org/content/act/en/products-and-services/the-act/test-preparation/description-of-science-test.html) emphasises interpretation, analysis, evaluation, reasoning and problem-solving. Efficient evidence use matters more than racing through scientific text.

## A one-week pacing reset

For each practice passage:

1. scan the structure and label the figures
2. answer evidence-location questions first
3. underline the exact words controlling each comparison
4. move on when a question exceeds the agreed limit
5. review whether errors came from speed or misunderstanding

Aim for controlled accuracy before compressing the time. A rushed wrong method does not become a right method through repetition.

If the greatest delays occur in visual questions, read [why ACT Science graphs and tables cause students problems](https://otalearningstudio.com/blog/act-science-data-interpretation-graphs-tables.html).

Parents can help by asking for a timing breakdown rather than only the unfinished total. If most time is lost on one passage type, the plan should target that type instead of repeating entire tests.

### Separate speed from hesitation

Review a recording sheet after practice. Mark questions where the student knew the method but moved slowly, questions where they searched in the wrong place and questions where they did not understand the task. Each needs a different response: fluency practice, better navigation or direct teaching. One overall time figure hides those differences.

Do not reduce time every session. Repeat a passage type until the method remains accurate, then tighten the limit gradually. The goal is automatic organisation, not frantic reading.
