Your P7 child may read fluently at home and still lose marks in SEAG English. Perhaps they rush past a detail, choose a plausible answer that the passage does not support or spend too long on the first questions. An online SEAG English tutor should find which of those things is happening before giving another full paper.

[SEAG's published dates](https://seagni.co.uk/) place Paper 1 on 14 November and Paper 2 on 21 November 2026. Registration is closed. Families looking for help in October need a short, specific preparation plan. There is still time to practise well, but the plan should protect the child's confidence and normal routine.

## Understand the format your child will face

The [official assessment guide](https://seagni.co.uk/guidance/assessment-guide) says the main test in each paper contains 28 English or Gaeilge questions and 28 Maths questions within 60 minutes. There is also a short untimed practice section which does not count towards the outcome. Use current official practice materials and answer sheets, not a mixture of unrelated transfer test books.

One question that takes much too long can affect time for the rest of the mixed paper. A tutor therefore needs to see how the pupil handles English within the whole assessment, while still teaching the particular reading or language skill they lack.

## Use an actual mistake to choose the lesson

Imagine a short passage saying, “Maya folded the invitation and placed it in her bag. She wanted to tell her grandmother the news herself.” A question asks why Maya did not phone immediately. “She had no signal” might sound possible, but the passage does not say that. “She wanted to tell her grandmother in person” is supported by the text.

This is an original teaching example, not a SEAG question. The lesson is to distinguish evidence from a guess. Ask your child to point to the exact words that support an answer and to explain why another option is tempting but unsupported. Then use a fresh paragraph to check whether they can repeat the method independently.

Other pupils struggle with the meaning of a word in context, punctuation or sentence structure. If the issue is vocabulary, use the surrounding sentence to test meaning instead of memorising a long list. If the issue is grammar, teach a rule briefly and make the pupil apply it in a new sentence. [Our SEAG English guide](seag-english-comprehension-grammar-vocabulary-2026.html) goes further into these areas.

## Practise timing without making every session a race

Start with accuracy on a small set. Record how long it takes and which questions prompt repeated rereading. Once a reliable method appears, add a time limit to a mixed section. A child who has not learned how to read the question will not be helped simply by a louder countdown.

Teach a practical decision for a difficult item: rule out what the passage contradicts, make the best supported choice if possible and move on rather than letting one question consume the paper. Review the answer afterwards. Use SEAG's [free official practice papers](https://seagni.co.uk/guidance/practice-materials) to check technique and familiarity with the answer sheet.

Keep feedback calm and specific. “You used evidence for four of these five answers” tells a pupil what to repeat. “You are bad at comprehension” tells them nothing useful. The purpose of tuition now is to improve decisions under the paper's conditions, not to cover every possible word before November.

## What to ask before booking

Ask the tutor to explain what they will review in the first lesson, how English practice will fit around Maths and how progress will be checked with unfamiliar questions. Bring an attempted official paper, including the choices the pupil changed or guessed. That is more useful than a score alone.
