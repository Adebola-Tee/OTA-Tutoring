Your child can differentiate a complicated expression but loses points on an AP Calculus free response question. The answer may be mathematically useful and still fail to answer the part that was asked.

Free response questions, usually called FRQs, assess decisions as well as techniques. A student has to identify the quantity, choose a method, show the necessary work and communicate a conclusion. Differentiating is sometimes only one step in that process.

## Read the instruction before choosing the tool

“Find,” “interpret,” “justify” and “approximate” ask for different work. A numerical answer does not automatically satisfy an instruction to justify. A derivative formula does not interpret its value in a real situation.

Before calculating, the student should underline the required action and identify the function or quantity involved. That brief pause can prevent an entire page of correct work on the wrong task.

For example, if a tank’s water volume is V(t), then V′(4) describes how quickly the volume is changing at time 4. Giving the volume V(4) instead answers a different question. Stating the derivative value without suitable units may also leave the interpretation incomplete.

## Use evidence to justify a conclusion

Suppose a question asks whether a function has a local maximum at a point. Finding that f′ equals zero is not, by itself, enough. A stationary point can be a minimum or neither.

One possible justification is that f′ changes from positive to negative across the point, so f changes from increasing to decreasing. The explanation should connect the observed sign change to the conclusion.

Writing “the graph goes down” can be ambiguous. Which graph? The function or its derivative? Naming the mathematical object makes the reasoning clearer to both the student and the reader.

## Tables and accumulation need interpretation

A question may give a table of rates and ask for an estimate of total change. The student needs to select an appropriate numerical approximation, show the setup and understand what the resulting quantity represents.

If the rate is measured in litres per minute and time in minutes, accumulated change is measured in litres. Multiplying, integrating or approximating is not the end of the answer when the task asks what the result means.

The [College Board AP Calculus AB assessment overview](https://apstudents.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-calculus-ab/assessment) explains the range of representations and conceptual tasks. Students in BC face these communication demands as well as additional course content.

## Mark one response carefully

Take a released question and its official scoring guidelines. Have the learner complete one part, then identify what each scoring requirement is asking them to demonstrate. Avoid treating the model solution as a script that must be copied word for word.

Ask whether the original response had the required setup, mathematical evidence and conclusion. Then rewrite only what was missing. A later question should test the same reasoning in a different context.
