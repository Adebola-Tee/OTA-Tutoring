Parents are often surprised when a child who performs strongly in Mathematics struggles with **IGCSE Physics calculations**. The arithmetic may be accurate, yet the Physics answer is wrong.

That happens because numerical Physics is not simply Mathematics with units attached.

## The student must model the situation first

Before any algebra, the student must decide what the question describes and which physical principle applies. A confident mathematician can manipulate the wrong equation perfectly.

Ask the student to describe the situation in words, identify the required quantity and predict the type of answer expected before calculating.

## Similar quantities are not interchangeable

Mass and weight, energy and power, speed and velocity, current and charge have mathematical relationships but different meanings. Confusing them leads to the wrong method.

Students should define each quantity, state its unit and explain how it behaves physically.

## Units carry information

Centimetres may need converting to metres; minutes to seconds; grams to kilograms. Prefixes and standard form can change an answer by factors of hundreds or thousands.

A unit check before substitution is faster than discovering the mistake at the end.

## Multi-step questions require planning

Some questions do not provide the value needed for the final equation. The student must calculate an intermediate quantity first.

Writing a short route—“find this, then use it to find that”—prevents random substitution.

## Explanations still matter

IGCSE Physics includes qualitative reasoning, graphs and practical questions. A student who focuses only on calculations may leave many marks unprotected.
