If your teenager is taking the PSAT this October, the useful question now is not how many practice tests they can finish. It is which mistakes they can still understand and correct before their school's test day. An **online PSAT tutor** should help make that decision rather than fill every evening with another paper.

College Board lists the [fall 2026 in-school testing window](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/dates-deadlines) as 1 to 30 October for SAT School Day, PSAT/NMSQT and PSAT 8/9. Confirm the particular assessment and date with your school. The window does not mean a student can independently choose any day within it.

## Start with one useful baseline

Use official practice material appropriate to the assessment your child is taking. Ask the student to complete a section independently, then review the thinking behind several answers. Include a question they answered correctly but guessed, because a right answer can hide uncertainty.

Separate errors into three groups: missing knowledge, misreading and difficulty working within the available time. A student who does not understand linear equations needs teaching. A student who understands them but overlooks a negative sign needs a checking habit. The same worksheet will not solve both problems.

Keep a short record of the question type, the mistaken step and the corrected reasoning. That gives a tutor something concrete to work from, and gives the student evidence that revision has a purpose.

## Choose two priorities for the remaining days

A limited plan is easier to act on. In maths, one priority might be translating a verbal relationship into an equation. In reading and writing, it could be explaining why the evidence supports one answer more closely than the others.

For example, after choosing an answer to a reading question, ask the learner to identify the exact phrase that supports it. If their explanation is simply that it sounds sensible, practise separating what the text says from what they already know about the topic.

In maths, work through one example together, then change a number or rearrange the wording. Can the learner still choose the method? If they can only repeat the original example, the next lesson should return to the underlying idea.

## Build a session that leaves something behind

A focused preparation session can begin with ten minutes revisiting an earlier mistake, continue with teaching and guided practice, and finish with a short independent task. Timing should come after the learner understands the method well enough to use it.

The tutor should explain what to practise between sessions. Four carefully chosen questions, with a reason for each, can be more useful than a long assignment completed on autopilot. We would also ask the learner to redo one earlier question without looking at the solution.

Avoid treating every dropped mark as an emergency. Look for a repeated pattern across several questions before making it the main target. One unusual mistake should not consume the entire preparation plan.

## Keep the school and practical details in view

Ask the school about the test arrangements, required equipment and any approved support. Use the relevant official guidance rather than assuming every SAT Suite assessment has identical arrangements. Leave time to become familiar with the actual testing interface and instructions.

Keep the last evening manageable. Organising materials, reviewing a brief error log and getting adequate rest are more realistic than trying to learn several new topics overnight. A parent can help by asking what the student now understands better, rather than repeatedly asking for a predicted score.

## What to ask an online PSAT tutor

Bring a recent practice attempt and ask which two areas the tutor would prioritise, how they will check independent understanding, and what preparation fits the time remaining. A useful answer should refer to your child's work rather than offer a guaranteed score increase.
