The first weeks of IB Maths can reveal an uncomfortable mismatch. A student who managed the previous course now spends an hour on two homework questions. Notes become longer, unfinished tasks accumulate and they start describing the entire subject as impossible.

Waiting until the first major examination can turn a repairable gap into a backlog. Early support should begin by finding the first point where understanding became unreliable, while the current course is still moving at a manageable distance ahead.

## Identify what changed at the transition

Earlier school success sometimes depended on practising a familiar question format. IB tasks can ask students to connect ideas, interpret information and explain why a method works. A learner may know individual techniques yet struggle to combine them.

There may also be an ordinary prerequisite weakness. Fractions, indices, algebraic rearrangement and function notation recur across later work. If one of these is insecure, new content feels harder than it would otherwise be.

Ask the student to bring a recent unfinished question. Instead of asking why they did not finish, ask them to show the last step they understood. That makes the conversation about a solvable obstacle rather than effort or character.

## Make a two-track plan

The learner needs to stay connected to school lessons while repairing earlier skills. Trying to finish all missing foundations before touching current work can make the backlog worse. Trying to follow current work without any repair has the opposite problem.

Use two small tracks. One deals with a prerequisite, such as function notation. The other supports the current unit, using that skill where possible. For instance, understanding what f(3) means can be taught alongside interpreting the function the class is currently studying.

Keep the plan narrow. A list of twenty weak topics can feel like a verdict. Two clear priorities are easier to start and review.

## Help the teacher see the difficulty

Contact the school with examples rather than a general report that your child is struggling. Ask which earlier skills need attention and what the learner should be able to do by the next assessment. Confirm the precise course: AA or AI, SL or HL.

The [IB’s mathematics course information](https://ibo.org/programmes/diploma-programme/curriculum/mathematics/) explains those options. The course and examination year should guide both the materials used and the expectations for independent work.

If the timetable or course choice needs discussing, raise it early with the school. A tutor can assess mathematical gaps, but the school should advise on deadlines and academic implications of changing course.

## Reduce the homework battle

Agree a reasonable attempt time for a difficult task. After that, the student should write the exact question they need to ask: “I can substitute the values, but I cannot rearrange this expression.” That is more useful than copying a solution after becoming exhausted.

Short retrieval practice can fit around existing homework. Ten minutes reviewing a skill learned earlier, without notes, can reveal whether it has become usable. Follow it with feedback; repeated unsuccessful attempts without teaching do not fix the problem.
