If your child will sit the **SEAG Entrance Assessment**, you may be wondering:

**When should we actually start preparing for the Maths?**

P6?

The summer before P7?

September?

Or only when practice-paper scores start looking worrying?

There is no magic month that suits every child.

The better question is:

**How strong are your child's Maths foundations right now?**

A confident pupil who is already secure across Key Stage 2 Maths may need far less preparation than a child who is still struggling with fractions, problem-solving or basic number work.

## P6: build the foundations, not the pressure

For many children, P6 is a good time to strengthen Maths without making every lesson feel like SEAG preparation.

This is the stage to notice whether your child is secure with areas such as:

* number operations
* fractions and decimals
* percentages
* measurement
* shape and space
* money
* interpreting data
* multi-step problems

The official SEAG assessment is based on the Northern Ireland Key Stage 2 curriculum, so strong foundations matter more than racing ahead into unfamiliar Maths.

Parents can see the official assessment areas in the [SEAG Assessment Guide](https://seagni.co.uk/guidance/assessment-guide).

If a child already has noticeable gaps in P6, addressing them early is usually much easier than trying to repair everything during the final weeks of P7.

## Summer before P7: a good time to become more structured

This does not need to mean spending the entire summer completing practice papers.

A better approach is light but regular preparation.

For example:

**one weak topic + some problem-solving + a small amount of mixed practice each week**

The aim is to enter P7 remembering the Maths already learned — rather than spending September trying to rebuild skills that disappeared over the summer.

## Start of P7: preparation should become targeted

Once P7 begins, the preparation needs to become more deliberate.

At this point, parents should know:

**Which Maths topics are secure?**

**Which ones are inconsistent?**

**Where does the child repeatedly lose marks?**

That is when tutoring can become particularly valuable.

Not because every P7 child automatically needs a tutor.

But because a good tutor can stop preparation becoming random.

Instead of doing ten different worksheets, the child may need two weeks focused specifically on fractions, factors and problem-solving.

Our guide on [the SEAG Maths topics P7 pupils should be revising](https://otalearningstudio.com/blog/seag-maths-topics-p7-child-should-revise-2026.html) can help you identify the main areas to check.

## What if you only start 6–8 weeks before SEAG?

You can still make meaningful progress.

But the approach needs to change.

There is less time for broad, slow revision.

The focus should be:

* the biggest Maths weaknesses
* questions that repeatedly cost marks
* mixed SEAG-style practice
* timing
* problem-solving
* correcting mistakes properly

For the **2026 SEAG Entrance Assessment**, Paper 1 takes place on **14 November** and Paper 2 on **21 November**. Each main paper contains **28 Maths and 28 English/Gaeilge questions in 60 minutes**.

So by the final weeks, a child needs more than topic knowledge.

They also need to use that knowledge accurately under time pressure.

## When does a P7 child actually need a SEAG Maths tutor?

Consider extra support when your child:

* repeatedly gets the same topics wrong
* understands examples but cannot solve questions independently
* is becoming frustrated or losing confidence
* struggles particularly with Maths word problems
* has practice-paper scores that are not improving
* needs more individual explanation than school preparation can provide

One-to-one support can be especially useful when the weakness is very specific.

A child who struggles only with fractions and ratio should not spend every tutoring session revising Maths they already understand.

## Starting earlier does not mean studying harder

This is important.

Starting SEAG preparation in P6 does **not** mean a child should spend 18 months under examination pressure.

Starting earlier simply gives you more room.

More room to explain something twice.

More room to take a break.

More room to build confidence.

More room to fix a difficult topic without worrying that the assessment is only three weeks away.

That is often the real advantage of beginning early.

## What if your child is already in P7 and you have not started?

Do not panic.

Start with a short diagnostic.

Give your child a mixture of Maths questions and look for patterns.

Then prioritise the **three areas costing the most marks**.

A focused eight weeks can be far more productive than months of random revision.
