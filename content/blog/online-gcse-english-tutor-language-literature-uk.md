“We need an **online GCSE English tutor**” is a reasonable search, but it does not yet tell a tutor what to teach. English Language and English Literature demand overlapping skills, but the papers, texts and types of answer differ. The first useful step is to identify which mark is being lost and why.

Bring a marked answer rather than only the grade. A student may understand a passage but give too little evidence. Another may remember quotations but struggle to build an argument. The teaching plan should respond to the actual answer on the page.

## Language or Literature: ask the right question first

For English Language, look at how the student handles unfamiliar texts, inference, analysis, comparison and writing for a purpose. For Literature, look at their set texts, knowledge of the question, use of quotations and ability to explain how an interpretation is supported.

The [AQA GCSE English Language specification](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/english/gcse/english-language-8700/specification/specification-at-a-glance) is one example of a board with its own paper structure. Your child's board may be different. Share the exact specification and a recent question before asking a tutor for “GCSE English” generally.

If the student writes three paragraphs about a technique but never answers the question, more terminology is unlikely to fix the problem. We would practise making a point that directly responds to the question, selecting a short piece of evidence and explaining what it shows.

## What should happen in the first lesson?

An effective first lesson can be small. The learner reads a short extract or revisits one text they know. We ask them to plan a response, write a paragraph and explain their choices. Then we give feedback on one or two priorities and ask them to improve the paragraph while the reasoning is still fresh.

The tutor can then set a narrow goal: “Use evidence that actually supports the point,” or “Explain the effect of a word without repeating the quotation.” These are more workable than “get a grade 7.” Progress should be checked against a new question, not only the same paragraph edited repeatedly.

Online lessons should involve the student doing the reading, planning and writing. A tutor sharing a polished model answer can be helpful, but the learner also needs to try a fresh answer without prompts.

## A practical home check

Ask your child to show you one marked response and the question above it. Can they underline the sentence that answers that question? Can they point to the evidence supporting their idea? If the answer contains lots of good words but no clear argument, you have found a useful starting point for tutoring.

Keep the teacher's comments. They may show a repeated issue across several tasks, such as limited analysis or unfinished writing. A tutor should use that pattern rather than start a generic revision course from page one.

## Before booking, ask these questions

- Which exam board and set texts does the tutor need to know?
- Will the learner write during the lesson and receive specific feedback?
- How will Language and Literature priorities be separated?
- What independent task will show that a new strategy has stuck?
