**Moles and stoichiometry** often feel like the point where IGCSE Chemistry turns into Mathematics. In reality, most mistakes happen because students cannot connect the numbers to the particles and reaction.

## The mole is treated as a formula

Students may memorise amount equals mass divided by molar mass without understanding that the mole measures a quantity of particles. When the wording changes, the formula no longer feels useful.

Start by identifying the substance and the quantity represented. Units—grams, moles, particles or concentration—tell the student what conversion is needed.

## The balanced equation is ignored

The coefficients in a balanced equation give the reacting ratio. A student who calculates the moles of one substance but skips this ratio will usually produce the wrong final amount.

Use a simple route:

1. balance the equation
2. calculate moles of the known substance
3. apply the equation ratio
4. convert to the requested quantity

## Too many question types are mixed too soon

Mass, solution, gas-volume, yield and purity problems use the same core reasoning but introduce different conversions. Secure one type before combining them.

## Answers are copied instead of reconstructed

After checking a solution, close it and repeat the method from a blank page. Later, complete a new question that uses the same reasoning.
