Your **IGCSE results are out**, and perhaps one grade is lower than expected.

For students and parents in Switzerland, the first reaction may be to request a remark immediately or start planning a retake. But neither option should be automatic.

The better question is: **why was the grade lower than expected, and what is the smartest next step?**

Cambridge IGCSE results for the June 2026 series were released on **18 August 2026**, while Pearson Edexcel International GCSE results were released on **20 August 2026**.

## First, check how close the result was

A student who narrowly missed the next grade boundary is in a very different position from someone who was several marks away.

Before making a decision, speak with your school or exams officer and check:

- the student's marks
- the grade boundaries
- performance across individual papers
- whether the result matches previous school performance
- whether the grade affects the student's next academic step

If the result looks genuinely unusual, asking about a review may be reasonable.

## Should you request an IGCSE remark?

The word **remark** is commonly used, although exam boards usually refer to the process as a **review of marking** or an **enquiry about results**.

Cambridge and Pearson Edexcel both provide post-results services through schools and examination centres.

A review may be worth considering if the result is unexpectedly low or very close to an important grade boundary.

But a review does not guarantee that the grade will increase.

## Should you retake the IGCSE?

A lower grade does not automatically mean a retake is necessary.

A retake may be more worthwhile if the grade:

- prevents entry into a preferred A Level, IB or other programme
- affects an important future academic pathway
- is significantly below the student's normal level
- is in a key subject such as Mathematics, English or Science

For some students, moving forward while strengthening the weak subject may be better than sitting the full examination again.

## Find out what actually went wrong

If a student does decide to retake, the preparation should not simply repeat what was done before.

First identify the real problem.

Was it:

- weak subject knowledge?
- poor exam technique?
- running out of time?
- misunderstanding command words?
- careless mistakes?
- difficulty applying knowledge to unfamiliar questions?

A student who knows the content but loses marks through exam technique needs a different plan from someone with major syllabus gaps.

## Do not leave post-results decisions too late

Families should speak with their school or examination centre as soon as possible because post-results services have deadlines.

For Pearson Edexcel International GCSE, several summer 2026 review-of-marking services have a deadline of **24 September 2026**.

Cambridge schools also work within specific post-results procedures and deadlines.

Even if you are unsure about requesting a review, find out what your options are early.

## Remark, retake or move forward?

There is no single answer for every student.

A remark may make sense for one student. A retake may be worthwhile for another. For someone else, the best decision may be to move forward while strengthening weaker areas.
