More practice is useful only when it changes the decision the learner makes next time. Repeating full papers, rewriting notes or collecting vocabulary can create activity without movement. A focused diagnosis gives each lesson a clear purpose.

For this learner, the central issue is **choosing an online programme that teaches transferable reasoning rather than tricks**. More specifically, families may pay for lessons without knowing how diagnosis, homework, feedback and progress will work. That distinction matters because a broad instruction such as “practise English” does not tell the learner what to do differently.

## What the task is really testing

The [College Board SAT Reading and Writing overview](https://satsuite.collegeboard.org/sat/whats-on-the-test/reading-writing) shows that learners must handle short digital passages, evidence, rhetoric and Standard English decisions. Success therefore depends on more than general fluency, memory or completing many questions.

The student must understand the task, select relevant information, make a controlled decision and show the reasoning clearly. When one link in that chain is missing, an answer can sound intelligent and still earn fewer marks than expected.

## What should change first?

Start with recent evidence: a marked script, a timed section, school writing or a recorded response. Label each weakness instead of circling the final score. Look for:

- misunderstanding the exact instruction
- selecting weak or irrelevant evidence
- stopping the explanation too early
- inaccurate grammar, vocabulary or expression
- poor organisation or weak paragraph purpose
- timing, hesitation or unfinished work

The first practical step is to **ask how the tutor will track domains, timing, accuracy and independent transfer**. Keep that priority stable long enough to test whether it improves fresh work.

## A practice cycle that produces progress

A focused cycle should include:

1. a short explanation or model of the skill
2. guided practice where the thinking is made visible
3. independent work without prompts
4. precise correction of the actual error
5. a delayed retest using new material

Many learners stop at step four. They understand the correction while looking at it, but they have not yet proved that they can transfer the method. The delayed retest is what turns feedback into learning.

## What parents should monitor

Ask what specific error is being reduced this week. “I studied English” is difficult to evaluate. “I can now distinguish a supported inference from an attractive assumption” or “I can plan a comparative paragraph before writing” shows a usable change.

Track accuracy, completion and the number of repeated errors across comparable tasks. A total score may fluctuate, but better decisions should become visible before the final result rises.
