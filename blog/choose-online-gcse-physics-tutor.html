GCSE Physics tutoring

How to choose an online GCSE Physics tutor

By Taiwo Oloyede19 October 20264 min read

A student can remember that energy is conserved and still struggle to explain where it goes in an unfamiliar situation. Another can recall an equation but cannot choose the quantities to substitute. A useful GCSE Physics tutor needs to hear those differences before deciding what to teach.

At OTA Learning Studio, we offer online Physics lessons through Google Meet. Book a free consultation to discuss the student's course and recent work, then consider available class packages, times and fees before paid sessions begin.

Check the course rather than the subject label

Confirm the board, tier and whether Physics is separate or part of Combined Science. The topic name alone does not establish the expected depth or the correct assessment resources.

AQA's GCSE Physics specification overview is one official reference. Other boards and Combined Science courses have their own specifications. Ask how the tutor checks these details and keeps practice matched to the student's actual entry.

A good provider should also ask what school is currently teaching. Lessons can repair a prerequisite while helping the learner stay connected with present classwork.

Listen for a complete scientific explanation

Consider an object moving at constant speed in a straight line. The resultant force is zero, although forces may still act and balance. A learner saying no force acts has missed an important distinction.

Ask how the tutor would investigate that answer. A force diagram and a discussion of opposing forces can help the student connect the statement with the situation. The explanation should make room for questions rather than simply replace one memorised sentence with another.

Then change the context. Can the student explain a vehicle travelling steadily when the driving force balances resistive forces? Transfer to a new example shows more than repeating the original wording.

Expect calculations to include interpretation

A tutor should inspect equation selection, units, rearrangement and the meaning of the answer. A correct number without a defensible method may hide a guess or a calculator entry that happened to work.

For example, power can be understood as energy transferred per unit time. If 600 joules are transferred in 20 seconds, the average power is 30 watts. Ask why division is appropriate and what the unit describes.

Our Physics calculations guide explains how unit decisions can affect the result. A student with strong school Maths may still need help choosing the physical model.

Ask about practical reasoning and written work

GCSE preparation should include analysing methods, graphs and evidence, not just recall and equations. Ask how the learner practises identifying variables, explaining a control or evaluating the effect of a measurement problem.

During an online lesson, the tutor should see the student's diagrams and responses. Clear paper or another suitable shared method can make that possible. A parent may help with equipment, then leave the child to explain their own reasoning.

An explanation task and a practical evaluation need different feedback. Ask for an example of how the tutor would identify what is missing from each type of response.

Choose a first priority you can review

A useful initial goal could be interpreting force diagrams, handling units consistently or writing a clearer explanation of energy transfer. Ask how that goal will be checked on unfamiliar questions and when the plan will be reconsidered.

Discuss lesson length, practice, fees and scheduling before booking. A friendly teaching relationship matters, but it should sit alongside evidence that the learner can do more independently.

Bring one recent question and the student's attempt to your consultation. We can discuss what kind of online support may fit and explain the available paid arrangements. You should understand what the first lessons would address, rather than be asked to buy a broad promise of better grades without a teaching plan.
