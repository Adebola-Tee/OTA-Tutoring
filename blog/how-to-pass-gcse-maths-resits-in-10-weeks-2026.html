How to Pass GCSE Maths Resits in 10 Weeks: 2026 Revision Plan

By Taiwo Oloyede1 September 202610 min read

If you need to pass a GCSE Maths resit in November 2026, the next ten weeks matter.

The good news is that you do not need to relearn two years of GCSE Maths from the beginning. A successful resit plan should identify exactly where you are losing marks, strengthen those areas and gradually move you towards completing full papers under exam conditions.

This 10-week GCSE Maths resit revision plan shows you what to focus on each week, whether you are retaking AQA, Pearson Edexcel or another GCSE Maths specification.

Can You Pass GCSE Maths in 10 Weeks?

Yes, it is possible to make significant progress in ten weeks.

But there is an important difference between studying Maths for ten weeks and preparing effectively for a GCSE Maths resit for ten weeks.

Simply watching revision videos, rereading notes or completing random questions is unlikely to be enough.

Your preparation should revolve around three things:

  • finding the topics costing you marks
  • practising those topics until you can answer questions independently
  • completing and analysing GCSE Maths past papers

If you are preparing for the November series, also check our November GCSE Maths Resit Dates and Entry Deadlines 2026 so that you do not miss an important registration or exam date.

Before Week 1: Find Out Why You Did Not Get Your Target Grade

Do not start by opening your textbook at Chapter 1.

Start with evidence.

Complete a GCSE Maths past paper under timed conditions and mark it carefully.

For every question you lose marks on, ask why.

Was it because:

  • you did not understand the topic?
  • you knew the method but forgot a step?
  • you made an arithmetic mistake?
  • you misunderstood the question?
  • you ran out of time?
  • you did not show enough working?

This creates your personal GCSE Maths resit revision list.

That list should determine what you study over the next ten weeks.

Week 1: Diagnose Your Weakest GCSE Maths Topics

Your first week is about finding the gaps.

Complete a timed paper from your exam board and mark it using the official mark scheme.

Then divide your mistakes into topics.

You may discover that most of your lost marks come from areas such as:

  • fractions, decimals and percentages
  • ratio and proportion
  • algebra
  • equations and inequalities
  • graphs
  • geometry
  • probability
  • statistics
  • multi-step problem-solving questions

Choose your weakest three to five areas.

Those topics become your priority.

Week 2: Fix Number, Fractions, Percentages and Ratio

For many GCSE Maths resit students, basic number skills affect marks across several parts of the examination.

This week, strengthen topics such as:

  • fractions
  • percentages
  • decimals
  • ratio
  • proportion
  • standard form
  • negative numbers
  • estimation

Do not just read worked examples.

Attempt questions yourself.

If you cannot complete a question without looking at the solution, you have not mastered the skill yet.

Week 3: Attack Your Weakest Algebra Topics

Algebra can quickly become a major source of lost marks, particularly for Higher Tier students.

Depending on your weaknesses, practise:

  • simplifying expressions
  • expanding brackets
  • factorising
  • solving equations
  • inequalities
  • sequences
  • simultaneous equations
  • rearranging formulae
  • algebraic fractions
  • quadratic equations

Focus first on the algebra topics that appeared in your Week 1 diagnosis.

The purpose of this plan is not to revise everything equally.

Week 4: Strengthen Geometry, Measures and Graphs

Now move to another major group of GCSE Maths topics.

Practise areas such as:

  • angles
  • area and perimeter
  • volume and surface area
  • Pythagoras' theorem
  • trigonometry
  • transformations
  • coordinates
  • straight-line graphs
  • speed, distance and time

Pay particular attention to questions that combine several skills.

These are often where students know the individual methods but struggle to work out what the question is asking them to do.

Week 5: Practise Probability and Statistics

By Week 5, you should be moving beyond isolated calculations.

Work on:

  • probability
  • frequency trees
  • Venn diagrams
  • averages
  • range
  • scatter graphs
  • cumulative frequency where appropriate
  • interpreting charts and data

Then mix these questions with topics from Weeks 2–4.

This is important because the real GCSE Maths paper will not tell you which topic you are about to use.

You need to recognise the method yourself.

Week 6: Start Serious GCSE Maths Past-Paper Practice

At this point, past papers should become a much bigger part of your GCSE Maths resit preparation.

Complete one paper under timed conditions.

Then mark it.

But do not stop after calculating your score.

Create a mistake list.

For every lost mark, write down:

  1. the topic
  2. what went wrong
  3. the correct method
  4. whether you could answer a similar question next time

Your mistakes are now telling you exactly what to revise.

Week 7: Turn Lost Marks Into Marks You Can Win

Return to your Week 6 paper.

Look for patterns.

Perhaps you repeatedly lose marks on percentages.

Perhaps algebra is improving but geometry is still weak.

Perhaps your calculations are correct but you lose marks because you do not show enough working.

This week is about turning those repeated weaknesses into reliable marks.

Do targeted questions during the week, followed by another timed paper at the weekend.

Compare the result with your previous paper.

Week 8: Practise Calculator and Non-Calculator Skills

For GCSE Maths, you need to be comfortable working both with and without your calculator.

For the November 2026 series, Maths Paper 1 is the non-calculator paper, followed by two calculator papers.

Do not make the mistake of practising almost everything with a calculator.

For non-calculator preparation, strengthen mental and written methods involving:

  • fractions
  • percentages
  • multiplication and division
  • powers and roots
  • estimation
  • algebraic manipulation

For calculator papers, practise using your calculator efficiently for multi-step questions rather than depending on it to decide which mathematical method you need.

Week 9: Complete Full GCSE Maths Papers Under Exam Conditions

Week 9 should feel much more like the real examination.

Choose a complete past paper.

Give yourself the correct amount of time.

No phone.

No notes.

No pausing to check a method online.

Complete the paper as if you were sitting the real GCSE Maths resit.

Then mark it carefully.

Ask yourself:

If this had been my real resit, where would I have lost unnecessary marks?

Those are your priorities for the final week.

Week 10: Stop Learning Everything and Polish What You Know

The final week is not the time to panic and try to learn the entire GCSE Maths syllabus again.

Concentrate on:

  • recurring mistakes from past papers
  • formulas and methods you frequently forget
  • weaker topics that can still realistically improve
  • showing clear working
  • checking calculations
  • interpreting questions correctly
  • managing your time

Complete shorter mixed-question sessions and review previous mistakes.

The goal is to enter the examination able to collect the marks you are capable of earning.

How Many Hours Should You Revise for a GCSE Maths Resit?

Consistency matters more than occasional marathon revision sessions.

A realistic routine might involve 45–60 minutes of focused Maths revision on most days, with longer sessions when completing full past papers.

For example:

  • Monday: weak topic practice
  • Tuesday: second weak topic
  • Wednesday: mixed exam questions
  • Thursday: return to previous mistakes
  • Friday: lighter review
  • Weekend: timed past paper and corrections

Even a strong revision timetable will fail if you spend most of the session passively watching someone else solve Maths problems.

You need to solve them yourself.

The Most Important GCSE Maths Resit Strategy: Keep a Mistake Book

One of the simplest ways to improve before a GCSE Maths resit is to keep a record of your mistakes.

Every time you lose a mark, record:

  • the topic
  • the question type
  • your mistake
  • the correct method

Then return to those questions several days later.

If you can now solve them independently, you have improved.

If you make the same mistake again, that topic still needs attention.

Your goal during these ten weeks should be to make your mistake list shorter and shorter.

What Should You Revise for a Grade 4 GCSE Maths Resit?

If your main target is a Grade 4, do not spend all your time struggling with the hardest questions on the paper.

Secure the accessible marks first.

Build confidence in core areas including number, fractions, percentages, ratio, basic algebra, equations, graphs, geometry, probability and statistics.

Accuracy on questions you can answer is extremely important.

Twenty avoidable mistakes across three papers can make an enormous difference to your final result.

Foundation or Higher for Your GCSE Maths Resit?

Your tier should match your target and current level.

Foundation Tier offers grades 1–5.

Higher Tier offers grades 4–9, with a limited Grade 3 available in certain circumstances.

If your main goal is achieving Grade 4 or Grade 5, do not automatically assume Higher Tier is the better choice.

Speak to your school, college, exam centre or Maths teacher about which tier gives you the most realistic route to your target grade.

Common GCSE Maths Resit Mistakes to Avoid

Even students who revise regularly can waste valuable preparation time.

Avoid:

  • revising topics you already know because they feel comfortable
  • watching videos without attempting questions afterwards
  • completing past papers without analysing mistakes
  • checking answers too quickly
  • ignoring non-calculator skills
  • leaving timed practice until the final week
  • trying to learn difficult topics while basic skills remain weak
  • giving up on a question without showing any working

Your revision needs to become increasingly question-based and exam-focused as November approaches.

What If You Are Starting With Less Than 10 Weeks?

Start anyway.

If you have eight, six or even four weeks remaining, compress the plan rather than abandoning it.

Begin with the diagnostic paper, identify your biggest weaknesses and prioritise the topics most likely to improve your score.

Can a GCSE Maths Tutor Help With a Resit?

A tutor can be particularly useful when you know you need a better GCSE Maths grade but are unsure why your previous revision did not work.

Effective resit tuition should not simply restart the GCSE Maths syllabus from the beginning.

It should help you:

  • diagnose gaps quickly
  • understand difficult topics
  • correct repeated mistakes
  • practise exam-style questions
  • improve exam technique
  • follow a structured plan before the resit

How to Pass Your GCSE Maths Resit: Final Advice

If you want to pass your GCSE Maths resit, do not spend the next ten weeks trying to become perfect at every topic.

Find the marks you are currently losing.

Fix the underlying weakness.

Practise the question again.

Then test yourself under exam conditions.

Repeat that process consistently and you will arrive at the resit much better prepared than you were the first time.

Your previous GCSE Maths result tells you where you started. It does not have to determine where you finish.
