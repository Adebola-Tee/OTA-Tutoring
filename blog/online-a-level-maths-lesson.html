Online A Level Maths lessons

What happens in an individual A Level Maths lesson

By Taiwo Oloyede19 October 20264 min read

An individual A Level Maths lesson should give the student room to think, not simply a front-row view of someone else's solution. Before booking, ask how the tutor moves from explanation to an attempt the learner can complete independently.

At OTA Learning Studio, we discuss your child's course and priorities before arranging paid Google Meet lessons. A free parent consultation lets you ask about the first teaching focus, available times and fees. This example shows what a purposeful calculus session could explore.

Begin with the student's interpretation

Suppose y = x³ − 3x. The task is to find the stationary points and determine their nature. Before differentiating, ask what stationary means on the graph. The gradient is zero at such a point; that does not mean the y-coordinate must be zero.

Differentiating gives dy/dx = 3x² − 3. Setting this equal to zero gives x² = 1, so x = −1 or x = 1. Substituting into the original function gives the points (−1, 2) and (1, −2).

If a learner substitutes into the derivative to find y, the tutor needs to revisit what each expression represents. That is a conceptual distinction, not merely an untidy calculation.

Explain the classification rather than announce it

The second derivative is 6x. At x = −1 it is negative, indicating a local maximum. At x = 1 it is positive, indicating a local minimum. A sketch can connect those signs to the behaviour of the curve.

Ask why this test applies here and what would happen if the second derivative were zero. It would not settle the classification by itself. A tutor should avoid presenting a method as an unconditional rule that works without checking its conditions.

A student who needs a simpler starting point may first explore a quadratic. A confident student might discuss changes in the derivative's sign or compare representations. Individual teaching should respond to the reasoning being heard.

Give the student a changed problem

Try y = x³ − 12x. The derivative vanishes at x = −2 and x = 2. Can the learner obtain the corresponding coordinates, classify them and explain the method without hints?

Keep the working visible. A correct final pair of coordinates can conceal a guessed classification or a copied procedure. Asking for a brief explanation helps distinguish secure understanding from familiarity with the previous example.

Later, mix a stationary-point task with a question asking where a function is increasing. The learner must decide what the derivative tells them in each case rather than automatically solve the last question type again.

Keep the lesson connected to the qualification

Confirm the exam board, specification and stage of study. Pearson's A Level Mathematics qualification page is a reference for that course; International A Levels and other boards need their own materials.

The same lesson structure can support statistics or mechanics, but the examples and prerequisites change. A probability lesson might inspect assumptions before selecting a distribution. A mechanics lesson might begin with a diagram before writing equations.

Leave with something clear to practise

A useful finish identifies what became clearer and what still needs attention. The student might receive a short practice task that includes one familiar structure and one changed application. A later independent attempt can reveal what has been retained.

Agree how written work will be shared online, which calculator is needed and how feedback will be used. Parents should understand the purpose of the proposed class package and the fee before booking.
