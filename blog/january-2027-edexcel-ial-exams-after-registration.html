International A Level Preparation

How to prepare for January 2027 Edexcel IAL exams after registration

By Taiwo Oloyede10 October 20264 min read

You have registered for the January 2027 Edexcel International A Level exams. Now comes the harder question: what should your child actually do each week? Opening a past paper is a start, but repeating papers without changing the underlying mistakes can make preparation feel busy rather than productive.

The first revision plan should fit the units entered, the student's recent work and their other commitments. At OTA Learning Studio, we use those details to discuss online Maths and Science tutoring that has a clear purpose from the beginning.

Put the entered units in front of you

Use the entry confirmation from the examination centre to list each subject and unit code. Then check the dates against the official Pearson January 2027 IAL timetable. Follow your centre's instructions for local reporting times. Do not assume every paper for a subject happens on the same day.

Distinguish a first attempt from a retake. A student learning a unit for the first time may still need teaching alongside revision. A retake student needs to find out why previous preparation did not translate into marks. Their old score alone cannot answer that question.

Use a small diagnostic before another full paper

Choose a few representative questions from the entered units and ask the student to attempt them independently. Keep the original working, including crossed out lines. Those details show whether the difficulty begins with knowledge, method selection, calculation or explanation.

For example, a Maths student may differentiate a polynomial correctly but struggle when asked to find where a curve is stationary. They need to connect the words to setting the derivative equal to zero. More practice at differentiation alone may leave that decision untouched.

In Chemistry, a student may recall a reaction but use the wrong mole ratio in a calculation. In Physics, they may know an equation but model the situation incorrectly. Write down the exact missing step rather than labelling the entire chapter a weakness.

Turn a weakness into a lesson goal

“Improve Pure Maths” is too broad for one session. “Recognise when to differentiate, solve the resulting equation and interpret the answer” gives both student and tutor something to check.

A useful sequence is explanation, a guided example, an independent question and feedback. Change the numbers or wording in the independent task so that the student must make the decision again. The aim is to transfer understanding beyond the example just completed together.

Ask the tutor what the learner will practise between sessions. A lesson should leave a manageable task and a reason for doing it. Our guide to January IAL Maths revision shows how to organise subject practice across the remaining months.

Allocate time by need and exam order

Start with the available evenings and weekends, then allow for school homework, other subjects and rest. Give recurring weaknesses regular attention without dropping topics the student already understands. A plan that requires several exhausting hours every night is unlikely to survive an ordinary school week.

As one example, a student could use a short session to repair a method, another to answer mixed questions and a third to review errors. The exact frequency depends on their entered units and current level. Revisit the plan when the work shows a new priority, rather than treating the first timetable as permanent.

Check progress through unfamiliar work

Every couple of weeks, return to a skill using a question the student has not memorised. Can they start without a hint? Does their working explain the method? Can they spot an unreasonable answer?

Later, timed sections and complete papers can test pace and endurance. Review unfinished questions separately from incorrect ones: running out of time and misunderstanding a concept need different responses. Keep the review specific enough to change the next week's practice.

Bring the right information to a consultation

You do not need a perfect revision plan before asking for help. Bring the entered unit codes, any previous results and two recent answers with the student's original working. Tell us which subject is most difficult and what lesson times would fit your family.
