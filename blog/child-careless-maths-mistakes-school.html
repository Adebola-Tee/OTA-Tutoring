School Maths accuracy

What Repeated Maths Mistakes Can Tell You About Your Child

By Taiwo Oloyede9 October 20264 min read

The answer is wrong by one digit. You tell your child to be more careful, and they promise they will. The same mistake appears again the next evening. When a reminder does not change the pattern, it is worth changing the investigation.

Careless is a description of how an error looks, not an explanation of why it happened. Some mistakes come from rushed recording; others reveal a misconception, a calculation gap or difficulty keeping track of several steps. The repair depends on which one you find.

Follow the working to the first change

Keep the original solution rather than erasing it immediately. Ask your child to point to the first line that differs from what they intended. Did they copy 46 as 64? Did they calculate 7 × 8 incorrectly? Or did they use addition for a situation involving equal groups?

These should not all receive the same advice. A copying error calls for a recording check. Uncertain multiplication needs practice with the fact and its meaning. Choosing the wrong operation needs work on the relationship in the question.

Let the learner explain before offering your diagnosis. They may have noticed a part of the task that is not obvious from the answer alone.

Distinguish a slip from a repeated belief

If a child writes 0.8 as smaller than 0.35 because 8 is smaller than 35, the mistake suggests misunderstanding decimal place value. Repeatedly saying read carefully will not teach the missing idea.

Use a number line or hundredths grid. Eight tenths is eighty hundredths, which is larger than thirty-five hundredths. Ask for another comparison afterwards, such as 0.6 and 0.42, to check the reasoning.

A slip looks different: the child explains the comparison correctly and solves fresh examples but records the wrong symbol once. That calls for a suitable checking habit rather than a complete new lesson.

Make checking specific enough to use

Check your work is too vague for many learners. Give a check that fits the calculation. For 398 + 205, estimate about 600. An answer of 503 should trigger another look because the estimate and calculation disagree.

For subtraction, use the inverse. If 72 − 28 is recorded as 54, adding 28 to 54 gives 82, not 72. The check points to a problem without an adult supplying the answer.

Choose one habit at a time. Asking a child to perform a long checking checklist on every small question can create more work than understanding. Aim for a brief, meaningful decision.

Reduce unnecessary memory demands

A crowded page can make it difficult to keep place. Encourage clear columns, one stage per line and a labelled sketch where useful. These are working habits, not evidence of a diagnosis.

Avoid insisting on mental calculation when the school expects written working for a multi-step task. The child should be able to record intermediate results instead of holding everything in mind.

The primary mathematics teaching guidance offers a useful reference for building number understanding. Match the level and method to the child's actual curriculum and the teacher's expectations.
