A Level Maths tutoring

How to choose an online A Level Maths tutor

By Taiwo Oloyede19 October 20264 min read

A student can earn a strong GCSE Maths grade and still need help when A Level questions combine several ideas. Choosing a tutor means finding someone who can locate that new difficulty and teach the connection, not merely demonstrate a longer calculation.

OTA Learning Studio offers online Maths lessons through Google Meet. Use a free parent consultation to discuss the exact qualification, a recent attempt and what support could focus on. We confirm suitable lesson arrangements and fees privately before paid sessions begin.

Establish which A Level the student is taking

Ask for the exam board, specification and stage of study. A UK A Level, an International A Level and Further Mathematics are not interchangeable courses. A Year 12 student learning differentiation needs a different sequence from someone revisiting an assessed International A Level unit.

For example, Pearson's UK A Level Mathematics page identifies that qualification. Share the school's actual course information rather than asking a tutor to infer it from a textbook cover or the phrase advanced maths.

A tutor should also ask which applied topics the school is teaching. Pure mathematics, statistics and mechanics can reveal different gaps even when the student's overall result looks consistent.

Check how prerequisite knowledge is investigated

Suppose a learner differentiates x² correctly but struggles with (2x + 1)³. The issue may involve the chain rule, algebraic structure or both. A useful explanation identifies the outer function and the changing inner expression before calculating 6(2x + 1)².

Then change the task. Can the student differentiate (5x − 4)² and explain the factor of five? Correctly copying the first solution does not answer that question.

Ask how the tutor handles an earlier algebra gap discovered during calculus. Ignoring it to keep moving through the syllabus can leave the learner repeatedly stuck at the same stage.

Expect reasoning alongside technique

A Level work asks students to interpret results and justify decisions. Finding a stationary point is different from explaining whether it is a maximum, minimum or neither. A tutor should connect the calculation with the behaviour of the function.

For y = x² − 6x + 8, the derivative is 2x − 6, which vanishes at x = 3. The curve opens upward, and its second derivative is positive, so this point is a minimum. The explanation gives meaning to the coordinate calculation.

A different student may need help choosing a probability model or drawing a force diagram. The tutor's plan should follow that evidence rather than assume calculus is always the first priority.

Find out what online participation looks like

Ask how written work is shared and how much the student attempts without hints. A lesson can use shared material while still making the learner's decisions visible. Continuous explanation from the tutor may feel fluent but reveal little about independent understanding.

Our guide to individual A Level Maths lessons illustrates a possible teaching sequence. A clear example is more informative than a promise to teach all boards and levels without discussing how the teaching changes.

Discuss manageable practice too. The next session should use that work to decide what needs revisiting, rather than simply move to a fresh chapter.

Ask what would count as a useful start

Choose an initial goal, such as recognising a composite function or forming an appropriate mechanics model. Agree how it will be checked on unfamiliar work and when the teaching plan will be reviewed.

Tell us the student's year, board, current topic and intended exams when enquiring. You can also ask about class packages, times in your region and payment arrangements. The first decision is whether our teaching approach fits the learner. A sensible plan should help your child understand both how to solve a problem and why that method belongs there.
