An online Year 5 maths tutor in Australia should make fractions click

By Taiwo Oloyede3 October 20264 min read

An online Year 5 maths tutor in Australia should be able to explain more than the rule for adding fractions. If a child says one quarter plus one quarter is two eighths, the issue may be the meaning of the denominator, not carelessness. A few thoughtful questions can show where to begin.

Ask the child to draw a rectangle divided into four equal parts and shade one part. Then shade another part in the same rectangle. How much of the whole is now shaded? They can see that one quarter plus one quarter is two quarters, or one half. The size of each part did not change when the number of shaded parts increased.

Use pictures, words and numbers together

Some learners can recite “keep the bottom number” without knowing why. Have them explain what a numerator counts and what a denominator names. Then ask whether two unequal pieces could fairly be called halves. The picture tests the meaning behind the language.

Move from a model to a number line. Mark zero, one half and one whole; ask where one quarter and three quarters belong. A child who can shade a pizza but cannot place the same fraction on a line may need help seeing a fraction as a number, not just a piece of food.

The Australian Curriculum Year 5 work sample on fractions shows representing and adding fractions alongside related number work. Schools and states may sequence teaching differently, so compare tutoring plans with the child's current classroom work and teacher feedback.

Find the first point of confusion

Try three short prompts: show three quarters with a drawing, put three quarters on a number line, then explain why three quarters is larger than one half. Listen to the explanation, not just the final answer.

If the child draws three shaded parts out of four correctly but puts three quarters near zero, the number line is the priority. If they cannot explain why one half is the same size as two quarters, equivalent fractions need attention before harder calculations.

One exercise should not become a diagnosis for the whole year. Use it to decide what to check next, and leave space for a child to correct themselves. A useful tutor will ask the learner to explain their choice and test another example rather than announce a long list of weaknesses.

What online lessons should look like

A shared whiteboard can work well when the pupil draws, labels and talks through each step. Watch who does the work. If only the tutor moves the shapes while the child says “yes,” it is difficult to know whether the idea has been understood.

At the end of a lesson, give a new example without hints. The learner might show one third plus one third with a diagram and explain why the answer is two thirds. A later lesson can introduce equivalent fractions when the basic idea is secure. Quick independent checks matter more than an impressive number of completed questions.

Parents can help with a short conversation about sharing, measuring or placing numbers between zero and one. Keep it friendly. If every meal becomes a test, the child may hide confusion rather than describe it.
