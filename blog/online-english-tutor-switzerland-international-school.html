Switzerland English Tutoring

Online English Tutor in Switzerland: Support for International School Learners

By Taiwo Oloyede30 September 20264 min read

Searching for an online English tutor in Switzerland can mean several different things. Your child may be learning English as an additional language, studying literature in English or using English across an international school curriculum. Those goals need different lessons, even when the timetable simply says English.

Before booking, gather the course name, a recent assignment and the teacher's feedback. A child who chats comfortably may still need help interpreting a text or building a written argument. A quieter learner may understand the work but need supported practice speaking in class.

Name the actual course and task

Ask whether the school teaches an IB, British, American or another programme, and which course the learner is taking. For an MYP student, distinguish language acquisition from language and literature. The IB's language acquisition guidance describes a progression through phases, so age alone is not enough to select suitable work.

Use the school's current expectations rather than a generic level label. A tutor should ask what the student is expected to read, write or discuss this term, and how the teacher assesses it.

Set a practical target: explain a viewpoint, write a paragraph with relevant evidence, or understand instructions more independently. That makes the purpose of extra lessons visible from the beginning.

Separate language from the subject idea

When a task is difficult, ask the learner to explain the idea in familiar language first. They may understand the concept but lack the vocabulary to express it in academic English. Alternatively, the vocabulary may be familiar while the reasoning is incomplete.

For a Science explanation, start with what happened and why, then add the necessary terms. For a text response, ask what the passage suggests before asking for a polished paragraph. This helps locate the obstacle without assuming every difficulty is an English language problem.

A tutor should teach the language needed for the task while checking the meaning. Memorising a sophisticated phrase is less useful if the student cannot choose when it applies.

Develop reading with a clear purpose

Choose a passage at a suitable level and agree what the learner is reading for. Are they identifying a main idea, evaluating a claim or explaining how a writer creates an impression? Those require different attention.

Ask the student to identify evidence and explain the link to their answer. If a response is broad, return to one sentence or detail in the text. This makes improvement concrete rather than simply asking for more analysis.

Vary the passage after teaching. The learner should be able to use the approach on fresh material, not only remember what the tutor said about the original extract.

Improve writing one decision at a time

Begin with the purpose, reader and main point. Then check the order of ideas and the evidence supporting them. Grammar matters, but correcting individual sentences cannot repair an argument that has no clear direction.

Work on one paragraph, discuss the change and ask for an independent second attempt. Keep the learner's own meaning and language visible. If every sentence is rewritten by an adult, the final version tells us little about the student's progress.

Feedback can name one strength and one next step. For example, the point is clear, but the quotation needs an explanation showing why it supports that point. The learner then has a specific action for the next task.

Make lessons fit the family and school

Agree a suitable slot in Swiss local time, materials to share and the amount of practice between sessions. Ask how the tutor will coordinate the work with current school demands. Extra support should be realistic alongside other subjects and activities.

Review progress through new tasks and fewer prompts, not just completed assignments. A parent should be able to see what the learner now does more independently.
