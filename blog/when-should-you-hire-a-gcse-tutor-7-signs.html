When Should You Hire a GCSE Tutor? 7 Signs Parents Shouldn’t Ignore

By Taiwo Oloyede20 September 20265 min read

Parents often wait until a mock result goes badly before thinking about tutoring.

Sometimes that is fine.

But often, the warning signs appear much earlier.

If your child is preparing for GCSEs, extra support is most useful before they become completely overwhelmed, not after months of struggling.

Here are seven signs worth paying attention to.

1. Their grades have stopped moving

Your child may be working hard, attending lessons and completing homework — yet their grades stay exactly the same.

That usually means effort is not the problem.

Something in the way they are learning, practising or answering exam questions needs to change.

For example, a student stuck around Grade 3 in GCSE Maths may keep revising but still lose marks on the same topics every week.

At that point, more revision alone may not be enough.

2. They keep saying, “I understand it in class, but I can’t do the questions”

This is one of the biggest signs.

Understanding a teacher's explanation is not the same as being able to solve a GCSE question independently.

Your child may know the topic but struggle to recognise:

which method to use what the question is really asking how to start how much working to show

That gap between knowing and applying is exactly where targeted tutoring can help.

3. Homework is becoming a battle

If every Maths, Science or English assignment ends in frustration, tears or arguments, something deeper may be happening.

The work may simply be too difficult because earlier foundations are missing.

A Year 11 student struggling with algebra may actually have gaps in fractions, negative numbers or basic equations from previous years.

A good tutor should find those earlier gaps rather than repeatedly teaching the current topic on top of weak foundations.

4. Their mock result was much lower than expected

One bad mock does not mean your child is going to fail GCSEs.

But it should not be ignored either.

Look beyond the grade.

Did they:

leave questions blank? run out of time? make repeated mistakes in one topic? misunderstand command words? * know the content but struggle under exam conditions?

5. Their confidence has dropped completely

Listen to the language your child uses.

“I hate Maths.”

“I’m just bad at English.”

“I’ll never get this.”

Those statements matter.

Sometimes repeated poor results make students stop trying before they even begin a question.

Tutoring can help here, but only when it is taught at the right level.

Giving a struggling student even harder work does not build confidence.

Helping them finally understand something that has confused them for months often does.

6. They are revising a lot, but the scores are not improving

This is a very common Year 11 problem.

A student spends hours:

watching revision videos highlighting notes rereading textbooks completing random worksheets

…but exam scores stay low.

Usually, the issue is not the number of hours.

It is that the revision is not targeted enough.

They may be spending time on topics they already understand while avoiding the exact areas costing them marks.

That is why the first few tutoring sessions should be diagnostic.

Find the gaps first.

Then build the lessons around them.

7. The GCSE exams are getting closer and there are still major gaps

Time matters.

A student with several weak topics in September has months to improve.

The same student discovering those gaps a few weeks before the exams has far less flexibility.

That does not mean parents should panic and suddenly book five lessons a week.

It means support should become focused and deliberate.

Our guide on how many hours of GCSE Maths tutoring a struggling student may need explains why the right amount of tuition depends on the student's actual gaps rather than a fixed number of hours.

So, when is the right time to hire a GCSE tutor?

Usually, it is when you can clearly see that your child is not closing the gap alone.

You do not have to wait for them to fail.

You also do not need tutoring simply because every other parent has arranged it.

Ask one question:

Is my child making the progress they need with the support they currently have?

If the answer is no, it is worth finding out why.
