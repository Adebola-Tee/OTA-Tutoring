Online Year 5 English Tutor: Better Writing and Reading Comprehension

By Taiwo Oloyede27 September 20263 min read

If you are looking for an online Year 5 English tutor, it helps to say what your child does when they read and write. “Needs better English” could describe a child who reads fluently but cannot infer meaning, a child with excellent spoken ideas but short written answers, or a child whose spelling makes drafting slow.

Use one reading passage and one writing sample

Bring a recent passage with the child's answers. Mark questions they could answer from explicit information and ones that required clues. Then bring a piece of writing, ideally one completed independently. Look for an idea the child tried to communicate before focusing on every spelling error.

The primary English curriculum in England connects reading, vocabulary, spoken language and writing. An international school may follow a different programme, so tell the tutor what the class is actually studying.

Reading: move from an answer to its evidence

Suppose a question asks why a character hesitates before entering a room. If the child says “because she is frightened,” ask them to point to two details that support the idea. A useful tutor can model one answer, invite the child to find a different clue, then give a new paragraph to practise.

For a child who reads the words accurately but cannot explain the meaning, our reading comprehension guide gives a short routine to try at home.

Writing: make one improvement at a time

Ask the child to say their idea aloud, write a paragraph and read it back. Can a reader tell what happened and why? The tutor might work on sentence clarity this week, then paragraph organisation next week. Correcting every mark at once can hide the more important issue: whether the child can develop an idea independently.

In a good online session, the pupil should do the reading, the talking and the writing. Shared documents can make revisions visible, but the tutor should not rewrite the assignment for them.

Before you book

Ask how the first lesson will identify a priority, how feedback will be given, and what the child can practise for ten minutes between sessions. A tutor should be able to explain the next skill without promising a particular test result.

If you have a Year 5 writing sample or comprehension exercise, bring it to a free consultation. We can discuss the most useful starting point for personalised online English lessons.

A question parents often ask: should we practise spelling or writing first?

It depends on what is stopping the child from expressing an idea. If spelling errors are frequent but the writing has a clear beginning, development and ending, a tutor can select a small spelling pattern without abandoning composition. If the child writes only two sentences because they do not know what to say, planning and spoken rehearsal may be more urgent.

Try asking the child to tell you a paragraph before writing it. Record three key points, then let them write without dictating every word. Compare what they said with what they wrote. That gap gives the tutor a useful starting point.

Keep the momentum going. A simple, consistent routine can make returning to school feel much more manageable.
