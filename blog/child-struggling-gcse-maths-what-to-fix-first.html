GCSE Maths Tutoring

How a GCSE Maths tutor identifies the topics costing your child marks

By Taiwo Oloyede28 September 20264 min read

Updated 16 October 2026.

A GCSE Maths tutor should use lost marks to investigate a learning need, not simply turn every wrong question into another worksheet. The useful question is where the student's reasoning first becomes uncertain. That might be an earlier number skill, the meaning of the problem or the choice of method.

Bring a marked paper with the original working still visible. Corrections are helpful, but an untouched attempt shows what the student did independently. A final grade provides context; the steps and omissions help the tutor decide what to check next.

Read the attempt before assigning a topic label

An incorrect answer to an area question does not always mean the student needs the area formula retaught. They might have selected the wrong measurement, mishandled a decimal or calculated perimeter instead. Those problems require different explanations even though the paper labels them under geometry.

Ask the learner to explain what they thought each number represented. Listen before correcting. A plausible method with one arithmetic slip needs a different response from a student who cannot describe the quantity being found. The first wrong step is often more useful than the final answer.

Use a short question to test a possible cause

Consider this original problem: a triangle has base 12 cm and perpendicular height 7 cm. The area is 1/2 × 12 × 7 = 42 square centimetres. A student obtaining 84 may have omitted the halving; another might use a sloping side because “height” is unclear.

The tutor could draw the corresponding rectangle to explain why the triangle occupies half its area. Then show a different orientation and ask the student to identify the perpendicular height. That checks the meaning, rather than only rehearse multiplication by one half.

Next, give an area of 45 square centimetres and base 10 cm. Can the student explain why the height is 9 cm? This reversal checks whether the relationship can be used flexibly. If necessary, teaching can return to the part the student cannot yet explain.

Look for patterns across more than one attempt

One response is a clue, not a diagnosis of the entire course. Check a few related tasks. If the learner makes decimal errors in area, money and percentages, arithmetic may be a shared priority. If calculations are secure but method choice fails, interpretation may need more attention.

Keep the record simple: topic, first uncertain step, teaching focus and a later independent check. Avoid declaring that every mistake is carelessness. A student cannot act on that label, while “identify which length is perpendicular to the base” gives a specific learning task.

Separate content gaps from assessment habits

Some students understand a method but lose track when several steps are required. Others omit units, round too early or cannot recover after a slip. Those habits need practice within meaningful questions, alongside any necessary content teaching.

Match the work to the confirmed qualification and tier. AQA's subject content is one official reference for its course. The tutor should use the learner's actual board rather than assume every GCSE resource covers the same content at the same demand.

Teach one priority and then test the explanation

After a focused explanation, reduce the help. The student needs a new task without the worked solution beside them. A correct answer with no prompts provides different evidence from a tutor led correction. Revisit the idea later to check whether it remains usable.

Turn the findings into a manageable plan

Choose a small number of priorities and explain how they connect with schoolwork. Keep some secure topics in mixed practice while repairing the weaker skill. Our progress review guide explains what parents can look for after teaching begins.
