IGCSE Physics Advice

Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625: Why Past Papers Aren’t Improving My Child’s Score

By Taiwo Oloyede22 September 20262 min read

Completing many Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 past papers can create the impression of serious revision. Yet some students keep receiving almost the same score.

The problem is usually not the papers. It is how they are being used.

A total score does not identify the weakness

After marking, classify every lost mark:

  • concept gap
  • incorrect equation or principle
  • unit, algebra or arithmetic error
  • practical or graph weakness
  • incomplete explanation
  • command word or timing problem

Count these patterns across two or three papers. That evidence should decide what happens next.

Reading the mark scheme is not correction

A model answer often looks obvious after it has been revealed. The student should explain what their own response lacked, close the mark scheme and rewrite the answer.

Several days later, use a fresh question testing the same skill. Correcting the original paper is useful; transferring the learning is the goal.

Full papers may give too little repetition

If a student is weak on circuits, moments or thermal Physics, one full paper may contain only a small number of relevant marks. Use targeted topic questions until the method becomes secure, then return to mixed timed practice.

Cambridge provides official 0625 past papers, examiner reports and specimen papers. Examiner reports are particularly useful for understanding common weaknesses, not merely checking final answers.

Support can hide the true score

Practice completed with notes, prompts or immediate solution checks is learning practice—not a reliable test. After support, students need a fresh closed-book attempt.
