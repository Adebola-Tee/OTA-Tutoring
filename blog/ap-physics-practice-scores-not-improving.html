AP Physics Practice Scores Not Improving? What Students Should Diagnose First

By Taiwo Oloyede23 September 20264 min read

When AP Physics practice scores are not improving, students often respond by completing more full tests. That may increase familiarity with the format, but it does not repair the weakness responsible for the repeated score.

A practice score is an outcome. The first task is to diagnose its causes.

Stop recording only the total

After each practice set, classify every lost point:

  • concept not understood
  • physical system or model chosen incorrectly
  • equation or conservation principle misapplied
  • algebra, calculus, vectors or units
  • graph or representation error
  • free-response justification incomplete
  • experimental design weak
  • time-management or reading mistake

Count these categories across two or three assessments. A repeated pattern matters more than a single difficult question.

Find the first wrong decision

The final line may contain an arithmetic error, but the solution may have already failed when the student drew the force diagram or defined the system.

Mark the earliest point where the reasoning became incorrect. That is usually the most useful teaching target.

Separate recognition from independent performance

Students often review solutions and feel that each step is clear. This is recognition. On the next test, they must generate the model and reasoning without seeing the route.

After reviewing a correction:

  1. close the solution
  2. reproduce the reasoning from a blank page
  3. explain why the method applies
  4. attempt a different question testing the same decision

The new question shows whether learning transferred.

Full tests may be too broad for repair

A complete practice exam may contain only one or two opportunities to practise the exact weak skill. Use targeted sets to build repetition, then return to mixed timed work.

For example, a student who mishandles energy-system definitions needs several carefully selected energy problems—not another exam that spreads attention across the whole course.

Compare multiple-choice and free-response performance

If multiple-choice performance is stronger, communication, justification or multi-step organisation may be the issue. If free-response reasoning is sound but multiple-choice accuracy is weak, pace, distractor analysis or rapid model selection may need attention.

The official College Board guide on how to practise for AP Exams points students to real questions, scoring information and sample responses. These resources are most valuable when used for diagnosis rather than repetition alone.

Check whether timing is the cause or the symptom

Slow work may come from weak recall, uncertain model selection or disorganised representations. Simply demanding greater speed can increase mistakes.

First stabilise the method on short sets. Then introduce time limits and track which stage consumes too long.

Set a narrow improvement target

Replace “raise my score” with a skill target:

  • justify conservation choices clearly
  • improve rotational dynamics model selection
  • label every graph correctly
  • complete experimental-design responses within a set time

Review the target with fresh questions after one week.

If formulas are the recurring problem in the algebra-based course, read why AP Physics 1 formula knowledge may still produce wrong answers.

Keep an error log that changes future practice

An error log should not become a list that is never revisited. Each entry needs the question type, first wrong decision, corrected principle and a date for retesting.

Group similar errors so patterns become obvious. Three missed questions about energy may actually share one issue: the system was never defined. Several rotation problems may trace back to an inconsistent sign convention.

At the end of each week, select two patterns and build the next practice set around them. Remove an error from the active list only after the student solves new examples correctly without support.

This turns past mistakes into a revision plan and prevents practice volume from hiding the fact that the same reasoning is failing repeatedly.
