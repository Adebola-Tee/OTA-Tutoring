HKDSE Biology Advice

HKDSE Biology Data Questions: Why Good Students Still Lose Marks

By Taiwo Oloyede23 September 20264 min read

Some students know their Biology thoroughly but lose a surprising number of marks on HKDSE Biology data questions. They recognise the topic, understand the experiment and may even predict the conclusion correctly.

The difficulty is turning a graph, table or unfamiliar investigation into the exact response requested.

Description and explanation are different

When asked to describe, students should report what the data shows. They need the overall pattern, relevant comparisons and, where appropriate, figures with units.

When asked to explain, they must use biological knowledge to account for that pattern.

A common mistake is explaining when the question asks for description, or repeating the pattern when reasoning is required.

Students often ignore the evidence

Answers such as “the rate increased” may be too general when data are available. A stronger response identifies the interval, compares suitable values and avoids claiming more precision than the graph supports.

Before writing, ask:

  • What is the overall trend?
  • Is there a maximum, minimum or plateau?
  • Are there anomalies?
  • Which two values provide the clearest comparison?
  • Are the axes linear, logarithmic or broken?

Correlation is mistaken for cause

Two variables changing together does not automatically prove that one caused the other. Students should distinguish what the data directly supports from what biological knowledge allows them to suggest.

If another variable may have influenced the result, that limitation should be acknowledged when the question asks for evaluation.

Unfamiliar contexts create panic

A data question may involve a species, condition or experimental setup the student has never seen. The unfamiliar details are often there to test whether the student can transfer a familiar principle.

Use a three-step method:

  1. Identify the biological topic beneath the context.
  2. Extract the relevant evidence.
  3. Link the evidence to the biological mechanism.

Practical design is part of data skill

Variables, controls, sample size, repeats, measurement accuracy and reliability affect how confidently a conclusion can be made.

Students should practise evaluating why a method may produce uncertain data and propose a specific improvement rather than writing “make it more accurate”.

The official HKEAA Biology subject information and resources should guide the style and level of preparation.

Use a correction table

After each practice question, record:

  • the instruction word
  • the evidence that should have been used
  • the biological principle required
  • the exact reason the mark was lost

Then answer a different question testing the same skill. This reveals whether the correction has transferred.

If content is secure but examination marks remain low, read why memorising HKDSE Biology notes is not enough.

Practise comparison language deliberately

Data answers often lose precision because students write “higher” or “lower” without naming the groups, conditions or time points being compared. They should state exactly what is compared and use figures only when they strengthen the point.

Useful language includes “increased from…to…”, “approximately twice”, “remained constant between…” and “the greatest difference occurred when…”. However, students must not force calculations when the question asks only for the main pattern.

Create short drills using one graph at a time: first write a description without explanation, then add a separate biological explanation. Keeping the tasks separate trains students to recognise the demand of the command word.

Finally, ask what conclusion cannot be made from the data. Recognising limitations is an important part of scientific judgement and prevents overclaiming.
