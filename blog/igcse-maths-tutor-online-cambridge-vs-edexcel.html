IGCSE Maths Advice

IGCSE Maths Tutor Online: Cambridge vs Edexcel Preparation Explained

By Taiwo Oloyede21 September 20265 min read

If you are looking for an online IGCSE Maths tutor, one of the first questions the tutor should ask is:

“Cambridge or Pearson Edexcel?”

Because although the Maths overlaps, the exams are not identical.

A student can be perfectly capable at Maths and still lose marks because their preparation does not match the paper structure, tier and question style of the qualification they are actually sitting.

That is why “IGCSE Maths tutoring” should never mean teaching every student from the same worksheet.

Cambridge IGCSE Maths: what is different?

For Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics (0580), students follow either the Core or Extended route.

Under the current syllabus:

Core candidates take Papers 1 and 3 Extended candidates take Papers 2 and 4 one paper at each tier is non-calculator the other requires a scientific calculator

Cambridge introduced the dedicated non-calculator papers from the 2025 syllabus onwards. See the current Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics syllabus.

That matters for tutoring.

A Cambridge student cannot rely on calculator methods for everything. They need strong arithmetic, algebraic manipulation and confidence working accurately without one.

What about Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Maths?

For Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1), students can also enter at Foundation or Higher tier.

The traditional linear qualification has two papers, each lasting 2 hours, and a calculator may be used in the examinations. Pearson also now offers a modular Mathematics A route for international schools outside the UK.

So already, the preparation strategy is different.

A student moving from Cambridge to Edexcel — or using resources from the wrong board — may notice familiar topics but a different assessment experience.

Does Cambridge or Edexcel have harder Maths?

Parents ask this a lot.

But it is usually the wrong question.

Neither board becomes easy simply because the paper format is different.

What matters is whether your child has been prepared for their specification.

For example, both qualifications cover major areas such as Number, Algebra, Geometry and Statistics.

But students need to become familiar with:

how their board phrases questions how much working is expected the correct tier calculator or non-calculator demands the length and pace of their papers the style of past-paper questions

A tutor who understands those differences can prepare the student much more precisely.

The exam board should be confirmed in the first lesson

If a parent tells us:

“My child is doing IGCSE Maths.”

that is not enough information yet.

We also want to know:

Which exam board?

Which syllabus or specification?

Core/Extended or Foundation/Higher?

What grade are they currently getting?

Which topics are causing problems?

That information changes what should happen next.

Our guide on what parents should expect from the first online GCSE Maths lesson explains why diagnostic work should come before simply working through a textbook.

Past papers should match the exam board

This sounds obvious, but students regularly mix resources.

A Cambridge student may find an Edexcel worksheet online and think:

“It is IGCSE Maths, so it must be the same.”

It can still be useful Maths practice.

But when examination preparation becomes serious, students should use board-specific past papers, mark schemes and specimen materials.

Cambridge provides official past papers and specimen materials for Mathematics 0580. Pearson also provides qualification materials for International GCSE Mathematics A.

That is how students learn not just the Maths, but how their Maths will actually be tested.

What should an online IGCSE Maths tutor actually do?

A useful tutor should not begin by teaching the whole syllabus from chapter one.

First identify:

topics already secure topics that are inconsistent genuine knowledge gaps careless errors timing problems exam-question weaknesses

Then build lessons around those findings.

One student may desperately need algebra.

Another may know the content but lose marks because their working is disorganised.

Another may be preparing for Cambridge Extended and need much stronger non-calculator fluency.

The label “IGCSE student” does not tell you enough.

The individual paper does.

How do you know if your child needs extra IGCSE Maths support?

Pay attention if your child:

understands lessons but struggles with past papers repeatedly loses marks on the same topics is unsure which paper or tier they are taking has mock grades below their target struggles under timed conditions has started losing confidence in Maths

At that point, targeted support can be much more useful than simply adding another revision book.
