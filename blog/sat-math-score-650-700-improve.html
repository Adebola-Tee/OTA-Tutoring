SAT Math score improvement

How to Improve an SAT Math Score Around 650 to 700

By Taiwo Oloyede6 October 20264 min read

A student scoring around 650 to 700 in SAT Math may know much of the content yet lose points in several different ways. A difficult function question, an overlooked restriction and a rushed answer can all appear in the same practice attempt.

The score range does not identify the cause. Before choosing revision topics, inspect the learner’s actual mistakes and the questions they answered correctly with uncertain reasoning. Those details are more informative than assuming every student in this band needs harder algebra.

Examine correct answers as well as wrong ones

Ask your child to explain a few questions they got right. Did they have a dependable method, eliminate options without understanding the relationship or guess? A correct answer can conceal a gap that will reappear in a different question.

Likewise, record why an incorrect answer occurred. A learner who understood the problem but mistyped a calculator expression needs different practice from one who never found a suitable equation.

Keep the original working. Reconstructing a neat solution afterwards can hide the decision that caused the error in the test.

Look for missed restrictions

Consider an equation involving a denominator x minus 2. The value x = 2 is excluded wherever it makes the original expression undefined. Clearing denominators does not remove that restriction.

Similarly, squaring both sides of an equation can introduce an extraneous solution. For example, square root of x equals minus 2 has no real solution, even though squaring suggests x = 4. Checking in the original equation exposes the problem.

Students sometimes lose these questions because their manipulation is fast but their checks are incomplete. Practise explaining allowed values before solving, then verify every candidate where the method requires it.

Strengthen flexible method choice

For one problem, factoring may be quick. For another, a graphing approach may reveal the relevant intersection. The learner needs to select a method that is efficient and produces the quantity requested.

Compare two routes on a small number of questions. What information does each show? Which is less vulnerable to error? Avoid forcing technology into every task or treating a longer handwritten method as automatically better.

Protect accuracy in both modules

The official SAT structure explains how performance in the first module influences the second module. Treat both seriously rather than assuming the first is only a warm-up.

Do not convert a predicted number of correct answers into a promised score. SAT scoring is more nuanced than a fixed points-per-question calculation. Use official practice results as feedback and examine the reasoning behind them.

If timing is the obstacle, practise a realistic module with new questions. Note where the learner spends too long and whether they can return to a difficult question within the same module.

Build a short priority list

Include questions the learner abandoned, not just those they submitted incorrectly. An unanswered problem may reveal an unfamiliar representation, a slow route or an idea that has never been taught securely. Ask for the first possible step before deciding that its only difficulty was time.

Choose the repeated errors most likely to benefit from teaching. That might be interpreting function notation, checking solutions after squaring or translating a parameter question. Make the list specific enough to test with fresh examples.
