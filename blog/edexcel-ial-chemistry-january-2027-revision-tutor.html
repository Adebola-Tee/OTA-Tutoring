January IAL Chemistry Preparation

How to revise Edexcel IAL Chemistry before the January 2027 exams

By Taiwo Oloyede5 October 20264 min read

Edexcel International A Level Chemistry revision needs more than recognising a page of notes. Students must select information, calculate accurately and explain chemical ideas with enough precision for the question. Three months before January is a useful point to check which of those tasks needs the most attention.

Start with the exact units entered and the 2018 Chemistry specification. Pearson also has a qualification labelled Chemistry 2027, but that label refers to the redeveloped course, not the January 2027 exam series. Pearson's transition dates list the January 2027 assessments under the existing qualification.

Separate recall from application

Choose a recent question and ask the student to explain the relevant idea before using the mark scheme. Knowing a definition and applying it to unfamiliar data are related but different skills. A correct memorised sentence may not answer the particular question.

For example, an explanation involving bonding should identify the particles and interactions relevant to the substance. Replacing all of that with “the bonds are stronger” can leave the examiner unsure what the student means.

After revising a concept, answer a fresh question that changes the substance, data or context. Check whether the explanation still makes sense. This helps prevent revision becoming a test of recognising yesterday's wording.

Keep the calculation connected to its units

Here is an original concentration example. A solution contains 0.0200 mol of a dissolved substance in 250 cm³. Convert the volume first: 250 cm³ = 0.250 dm³. Using concentration = amount ÷ volume gives 0.0200 ÷ 0.250 = 0.0800 mol dm⁻³.

Dividing by 250 without conversion gives the wrong scale for that unit. Writing the equation, conversion and final unit makes the reasoning visible and gives the student a way to check the answer.

Then change the task. A 100 cm³ solution has concentration 0.150 mol dm⁻³. The amount is concentration × volume, so 0.150 × 0.100 = 0.0150 mol. Ask the learner to explain why multiplication is needed this time.

These are practice examples, not predictions of an exam question. Use them to check understanding before moving to official questions from the units being assessed.

Practise explaining experimental decisions

Practical skills need deliberate revision alongside theory. Depending on the entered unit, students may need to reason about apparatus, measurements, observations, processing results or improvements. Reading a method is not the same as explaining why a step matters.

Take an experiment from your specification and identify the measured quantity, the equipment used and a plausible source of error. Explain how the error would affect the result where the information supports that conclusion.

Avoid generic improvements such as “be more careful”. A useful answer names a specific change and connects it to measurement quality. Follow the question carefully: an uncertainty calculation and a discussion of reliability are not interchangeable tasks.

Build October and November around your weakest tasks

Use short sets that mix calculations with explanations. Mark them using the correct official scheme and record the cause of each error. Was the issue chemistry, arithmetic, units or an incomplete statement?

Return to corrected tasks after a delay with different values or wording. As the methods become more secure, practise complete timed papers. Reserve time to review them; simply counting completed papers does not show what has improved.

Confirm dates and entry arrangements with your centre. Our Hong Kong January registration guide provides context, but your centre must confirm your individual papers and any administrative questions.

Bring a real answer to a consultation

If you are looking for online IAL Chemistry support, book a free parent or student consultation with the unit codes, a recent paper and one answer you cannot improve alone. We can discuss the learner's needs and an appropriate next step before paid tuition is arranged.

The useful question is not how many chapters a lesson can cover. It is whether the student can explain the chemistry, handle a new calculation and apply feedback independently when the next question changes.
