Japan International School English

Online English tutoring for children in Japanese international schools

By Taiwo Oloyede10 October 20264 min read

Searching for an online English tutor for a child at an international school in Japan can produce plenty of conversation lessons. But a parent may need something more specific: help understanding a reading passage, explaining an answer or completing school writing independently.

Before choosing lessons, identify the task that is difficult. A child who speaks comfortably may still need support with written reasoning. A quieter learner may understand the text but struggle to express that understanding in class. The right lesson begins with their work, rather than an assumption about their fluency.

Match the school's course and expectations

International schools do not all follow the same English curriculum. Ask the school which programme, year or grade expectations and assessment criteria apply. Share that information with the tutor, along with teacher feedback and a recent task.

For a child following an English national curriculum, the official English programmes of study provide a reference for reading and writing expectations. They should not be treated as the curriculum for every international school in Japan. The learner's actual course remains the guide.

Also distinguish school English from English as an additional language support. Some children need help accessing lesson vocabulary; others need more developed analysis or writing. Those needs can overlap, but they are not interchangeable.

Bring one reading answer to the first lesson

Try this original passage: “The playground emptied as rain tapped the windows. Hana stayed by the gate, watching the road and checking her watch.” Ask what suggests that Hana is waiting for someone.

Watching the road and checking the time support that inference. The passage does not prove who she is waiting for or why they are late. Have the learner separate what is stated, what is suggested and what remains unknown.

A tutor should ask for the child's reasoning before improving the answer. A copied model response may look impressive while concealing the same difficulty. Then use a fresh passage to check whether the learner can select evidence without prompts.

Improve the answer without taking over the writing

A student might write, “Hana is waiting because she is there.” The next step is to make the evidence specific: “Hana seems to be waiting for someone because she watches the road and repeatedly checks the time.”

Discuss why that version is clearer. It names an interpretation and connects it to actions from the passage. More advanced learners can consider alternative explanations or the effect of particular wording, where their course requires it.

Let the child write and revise their own answer. The tutor's role is to teach the decisions behind the sentence, not to complete assessed schoolwork for them. Keep a short record of the feedback they can apply next time.

Check vocabulary and sentence meaning together

Ask the learner to explain a key word in context, then use it in a different sentence. Recognising a translation or definition once does not show whether they can use it in a school explanation.

For writing, focus on one meaningful improvement at a time: a clearer reference, a precise verb or a sentence that connects evidence to an idea. Avoid replacing ordinary words with complicated vocabulary merely to make the work sound advanced. Clear meaning comes first.

Ask what the online lesson will look like

Look for a balance of reading, discussion, independent writing and feedback. Ask how the tutor will see the child's working and check understanding during the session. A long presentation with little pupil participation provides limited evidence of what the learner can do.

Confirm lesson times in Japan time, the expected practice between sessions and how parents will receive progress updates. Our guide to school writing and speaking support in Japan explains how the routine can fit everyday school tasks.

Decide whether the support fits your child

Bring the school's curriculum details, teacher comments and a recent reading or writing attempt. Tell us the learner's year or grade and whether the main concern is comprehension, sentence clarity or explaining ideas independently.
