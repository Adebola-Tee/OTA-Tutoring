IB Maths AI tutoring

How to choose an IB Maths AI tutor for your child

By Taiwo Oloyede19 October 20264 min read

A calculator can produce a regression equation in seconds. Understanding whether that equation is useful, what its parameters mean and where a prediction becomes questionable takes more thought. That is an important distinction when choosing an IB Maths AI tutor.

Here is what to look for beyond confident calculator demonstrations.

Match the course and cohort

Applications and interpretation is available at SL and HL. The level changes the content and demands, so the tutor should confirm it before selecting questions. Share the examination session and the school's current sequence too.

The IB mathematics course overview also identifies revised courses first assessed in 2029. Those changes should not be applied indiscriminately to a learner taking an earlier assessment. A clear plan starts with the version the student is actually studying.

If the student is unsure whether their school calls the course AI or AA, check the official course details rather than infer the answer from the amount of statistics in a homework task.

Ask for meaning alongside the output

Imagine a simple model for a delivery charge: C = 4 + 1.5d, where C is the cost in dollars and d is distance in kilometres. The fixed charge is $4, and each additional kilometre adds $1.50. A journey of 6 kilometres is modelled at $13.

The learner should explain those statements, not just substitute numbers. They should also ask where the model applies. A real service might add a surcharge or change its rate beyond a stated distance. A mathematical prediction is only as useful as its assumptions and context.

A lesson can extend this by comparing predicted and observed costs, discussing discrepancies and deciding whether a straight-line relationship remains sensible. The technology should support that reasoning rather than conceal it.

See what happens when the example changes

Ask the learner what the model predicts at zero distance and whether that makes sense for the situation. Then change the fixed charge while keeping the rate the same. Which feature of the graph changes, and which does not?

These questions reveal whether the student connects the equation, graph and story. If the only response is another calculator entry, the interpretation still needs teaching.

For a different learner, the priority might be probability, distributions or calculus. A tutor should investigate the actual attempt rather than assume every AI difficulty concerns regression.

Make calculator choices visible

The tutor needs to know which permitted technology the student uses and how they record a method and interpret a display. Ask how errors in data entry or mode settings are checked.

A well-organised lesson can begin with an estimate or sketch before using technology. Afterwards, the learner checks the result against the original question and states an answer in context. Written working and explanation remain valuable even when computation is efficient.

Our guide to IB Maths AI when the student is struggling explores how different weaknesses can lead to similar disappointing results. A targeted plan should address the cause rather than add more calculator exercises automatically.

Know what you are booking

Ask how the learner will show work online, what manageable practice follows and when progress will be reviewed. Fresh tasks should test whether a model can be selected and interpreted without hints. A repeated familiar example is not enough evidence of independent understanding.

For assessed exploration work, the student's thinking and writing must remain their own. We can discuss appropriate teaching support without promising to produce an internal assessment. Bring the course details and one recent attempt to the consultation so we can explain an available lesson plan, schedule and fee that you can assess before enrolling.
