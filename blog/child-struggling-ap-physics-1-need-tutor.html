AP Physics Advice

My Child Is Struggling With AP Physics 1 — Should We Get a Tutor?

By Taiwo Oloyede23 September 20264 min read

AP Physics 1 can surprise students who previously performed well in science and mathematics. They may follow classroom examples, know the formulas and still struggle when a test presents a new physical situation.

That does not automatically mean your child needs an AP Physics 1 tutor. It does mean the cause should be diagnosed before the gaps become larger.

Why AP Physics 1 can feel different

The course is algebra-based, but successful problem solving requires more than algebra. Students must model physical situations, use representations, justify reasoning and connect concepts across mechanics, energy, momentum, rotation and other course areas.

College Board explains that AP Physics 1 assesses scientific concepts and algebraic problem solving. A student who has prepared mainly by memorising equations may therefore feel capable during routine homework but exposed on unfamiliar questions.

Signs that tutoring may be useful

Look for a persistent pattern:

  • your child understands lessons but cannot begin new problems
  • equations are remembered but selected incorrectly
  • diagrams, graphs and written explanations are weak
  • corrections make sense, but the same errors return
  • free-response questions are regularly left incomplete
  • practice scores remain flat despite genuine effort
  • confidence is falling and Physics work is increasingly avoided

One low quiz is not enough. Repeated effort without measurable improvement is the stronger signal.

Find out whether the problem is Physics or mathematics

Use a recent test and classify every lost point:

  • concept not understood
  • incorrect model or principle
  • algebra, vectors or proportional reasoning
  • graph or representation error
  • explanation or justification incomplete
  • timing or question interpretation

A student who cannot explain Newton’s laws needs different support from one who understands the laws but mishandles vector components.

What effective AP Physics tutoring should include

Tutoring should not become a second lecture. Strong support asks the student to make decisions and explain them.

A productive lesson normally includes:

  1. diagnosis using current work
  2. concise repair of the weak concept
  3. guided modelling of a physical situation
  4. independent questions with unfamiliar features
  5. detailed correction of reasoning and communication
  6. later testing without prompts

The student should gradually depend less on the tutor.

Questions to ask before choosing support

Ask how the tutor will distinguish conceptual gaps from mathematical errors, how free-response reasoning will be taught and how progress will be measured.

The answer should refer to new independent work, not simply completed hours or chapters covered.

When tutoring may not be necessary

A short-term issue may improve through teacher office hours, an organised error log and two weeks of focused practice. Set one measurable target, such as improving energy FRQs or explaining force diagrams accurately, then review fresh evidence.

If formulas are known but answers are still wrong, read why AP Physics 1 formula knowledge does not guarantee the correct answer.

What parents can do before arranging lessons

Ask your child to choose one recently missed problem and explain where they first became uncertain. Do not focus immediately on the final arithmetic. Listen for whether the difficulty began with interpreting the situation, drawing a representation, selecting a principle or handling the mathematics.

Then ask them to solve a similar problem without notes after reviewing the correction. If the same decision fails again, the weakness is persistent enough to deserve focused attention.

Collect two or three marked assessments before speaking with a tutor. This gives a clearer picture than reporting only the overall grade and makes the consultation more useful.

The aim is not to wait for a crisis. It is to intervene when the evidence shows that the student’s current method is no longer producing progress.
