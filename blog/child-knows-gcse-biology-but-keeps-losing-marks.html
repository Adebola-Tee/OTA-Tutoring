GCSE Biology Advice

My Child Knows GCSE Biology but Keeps Losing Marks — Here’s Why

By Taiwo Oloyede21 September 20266 min read

Your child revises GCSE Biology and seems to know the content.

Ask them about enzymes, cells or homeostasis and they can explain it.

Then the test comes back and the grade is nowhere near what you expected.

This is frustrating — but it often means something very specific:

Your child may know the Biology, but they are not yet turning that knowledge into exam marks.

And that is a very different problem from simply “not revising enough”.

GCSE Biology is not just a memory test

This is where many students get caught out.

For AQA GCSE Biology, approximately 40% of the assessment rewards knowledge and understanding, 40% assesses application, and another 20% involves analysing information, evaluating and drawing conclusions.

Official AQA GCSE Biology assessment information

In other words, memorising the textbook can only take a student so far.

They also need to recognise which Biology idea applies when the question is presented in a completely unfamiliar context.

That is why a child can know a topic well and still lose marks.

They are answering the topic — not the question

Imagine the question is about osmosis.

Your child recognises osmosis and immediately writes everything they remember about it.

The problem?

The examiner did not ask:

“Tell me everything you know about osmosis.”

They may have asked your child to explain, compare, calculate or suggest something specific.

Command words matter.

“Describe” does not ask for exactly the same response as “Explain”.

“Compare” requires both sides of the comparison.

“Evaluate” needs much more than simply describing what happened.

AQA publishes specific definitions of the command words used across GCSE Science. See the official AQA Science command words.

Teaching students to pause and identify the command word before answering can recover marks surprisingly quickly.

Their answers are scientifically correct — but too vague

This is probably one of the most frustrating situations.

Your child reads the mark scheme and says:

“But that’s what I meant!”

Unfortunately, the examiner can only mark what was actually written.

For example, saying:

“More energy is produced.”

may not be precise enough if the question requires an explanation involving respiration, glucose, oxygen or energy transfer.

Biology answers need the right scientific language.

Encourage your child to ask after every longer answer:

“Have I actually explained why?”

That one question can improve written responses enormously.

Practical questions may be quietly taking marks away

Students sometimes treat required practicals as experiments they completed months ago and no longer need to think about.

That is a mistake.

For AQA GCSE Biology, questions drawing on practical knowledge and understanding make up at least 15% of the qualification marks.

Students may need to deal with:

variables and controls improving an investigation interpreting results graphs and tables sources of error conclusions and evaluation

So your child may know photosynthesis perfectly but still lose marks when asked how to investigate the effect of light intensity on its rate.

Knowing the Biology and knowing how to investigate the Biology are not identical skills.

Data and graph questions catch good students out

Biology contains more Maths than many students expect.

They may need to work with percentages, ratios, averages, magnification, probability and graphs.

AQA also expects students to interpret graphs, calculate gradients, handle data and translate between numerical and graphical information.

So when your child receives a lower Biology score than expected, look closely at where the marks disappeared.

Was the Biology actually wrong?

Or was it the graph?

The percentage calculation?

The magnification?

The interpretation of experimental data?

That difference matters.

They may know topics individually but struggle when topics are mixed

At home, revision often looks like this:

Today: Cell Biology.

Tomorrow: Infection and Response.

The student already knows which topic each question belongs to.

The exam does not always make it that obvious.

A question may combine several ideas or place familiar Biology inside an unfamiliar scenario.

That means revision should eventually include mixed exam questions, where the student has to identify what knowledge is relevant without being told.

That is a much stronger test of whether the topic is truly secure.

They are losing easy marks through exam habits

Sometimes the knowledge is genuinely there.

The grade is being damaged by things such as misreading the question, missing a key word, leaving an answer incomplete, failing to use information from a graph or writing far more than the question requires.

Take one recent Biology paper and label every lost mark:

Knowledge gap

Couldn’t apply the knowledge

Vague explanation

Practical/data question

Maths mistake

Misread the question

You will often discover that the problem is much narrower than:

“My child is bad at Biology.”

What should parents do next?

Do not immediately tell your child to revise more.

Get the latest Biology paper.

Find the pattern.

If the majority of marks were lost because the student genuinely did not know the content, reteach those topics.

But if they knew the content and still lost marks, the next stage of preparation should focus much more heavily on exam questions, command words, practical skills, data interpretation and precise explanations.
