What to Practise First in SAT Advanced Math

By Taiwo Oloyede6 October 20264 min read

Advanced Math can sound like a reason to start an entirely new revision course. In SAT preparation, a more useful approach is to inspect the particular nonlinear relationships your child finds difficult and work on their dependencies.

A weakness in factoring may affect quadratic equations. Unclear exponent rules may interfere with exponential expressions. A student who calculates well may still struggle with the meaning of a function parameter. These are different starting points.

Use the official domain rather than the label alone

The College Board SAT Math content overview identifies Advanced Math as one of the four domains. Review the current skills alongside actual missed questions rather than assuming the name refers to calculus.

The purpose is to locate a small group of relationships that need attention. Record the mathematical skill and the form of the question. Solving a quadratic and interpreting a quadratic model can expose different difficulties.

Read the structure of a quadratic

Consider x squared minus 5x plus 6. It factors as (x minus 2)(x minus 3). Setting the expression equal to zero gives roots 2 and 3, while the expanded form makes the constant term easy to identify.

Ask what each form makes visible. Factored form is useful for zeros. A completed square form can make a turning point easier to read. Being able to move between forms matters more than treating one form as universally best.

If the learner cannot factor the example, repair that skill first. If they factor accurately but cannot explain what the roots mean on a graph, teach the connection between the equation and function.

Check restrictions in rational expressions

For (x squared minus 4) divided by (x minus 2), factoring and cancelling gives x + 2 where x is not 2. The original expression remains undefined at x = 2.

This restriction belongs with the simplification. A learner who discards it has lost information about the original function. Ask them to identify the excluded input before cancelling anything.

Then give an expression with a different factor in the denominator. The check should reveal whether they understand the restriction or merely remember that one example excluded 2.

Separate solving from checking

Some operations can produce candidate answers that need verification. Squaring can introduce extraneous solutions; multiplying by a variable expression requires attention to its zero values.

After finding candidates, substitute where necessary into the original equation. Explain why that check is needed for the method used. A vague instruction to always check every line can make practice slow without repairing the specific risk.

Choose practice that builds independence

Start with a few related questions while the missing idea is taught. Then mix them with other topics. The learner must recognise when factoring, a graph, an equation rewrite or another approach is useful without a heading telling them the method.
