Does your child need Combined Science or separate Physics tutoring

By Taiwo Oloyede19 October 20264 min read

A parent says their child needs Physics tutoring. Before arranging lessons, there is one important check: are they studying Physics within Combined Science, or taking it as a separate GCSE? Both involve real Physics, but the qualification determines which material and assessment demands the tutor should prepare for.

At OTA Learning Studio, our free parent consultation begins with the course and the learner's actual work. We discuss paid Google Meet lessons, suitable class packages, available times and fees before booking. You do not need to know every specification detail yourself; the school can confirm the entry.

Get the course name and code

Ask for the board, full qualification and tier. For example, AQA Combined Science Trilogy and AQA GCSE Physics are different specifications. The word triple is often used informally, so the official entry is the safer guide.

AQA's Combined Science Physics content identifies its required material and flags Higher-only content. A tutor should use the specification relevant to your child rather than assume a worksheet suits everyone because the heading says forces.

If the school follows Pearson Edexcel, OCR or another course, share those details. International GCSE is another qualification and needs its own confirmation rather than being grouped with UK GCSE automatically.

Understand overlap without assuming identical coverage

Many ideas are shared across Combined Science and separate Physics. That makes some teaching examples useful for both, but it does not make the complete courses identical.

For example, AQA separate Physics includes Space Physics as a distinct topic, while its Combined Science Trilogy Physics content does not contain that same separate topic. Other differences and Higher requirements need checking in the relevant specification.

The tutor's plan should therefore distinguish a shared foundation from material included only in a particular entry. A learner should not spend scarce revision time on content outside their confirmed course while important assessed ideas remain insecure.

Choose a starting priority from the response

Suppose the student is asked why a moving object slows when there is a resultant force opposing its motion. An answer such as because the forces are balanced reveals a conceptual problem that deserves attention regardless of the course label.

A lesson can use a force diagram to distinguish balanced from unbalanced forces and connect the resultant with a change in motion. The tutor then checks a new situation rather than asking the student to memorise one corrected sentence.

For another learner, the issue may be equation selection or interpreting a graph. Our GCSE Physics lesson example shows how concepts, calculation and practical reasoning can be connected online.

Decide whether support is for Physics alone

A Combined Science student may need extra teaching mainly in Physics, or may have gaps across Biology, Chemistry and Physics. Discuss the actual pattern rather than assume the whole course requires equal additional time.

Ask how progress will be reviewed alongside schoolwork. An improvement in one topic is useful evidence, but it is not a guarantee of the overall Combined Science result. The plan should remain clear about what the lessons are addressing.

If other subjects need support, confirm the available teaching arrangements and fee before expanding a package. Parents should understand which subjects and sessions are included.

Book against the confirmed entry

Bring the course name, tier, current topic and one recent attempted question to the consultation. A photo of the relevant school course information can help when the naming is unclear.

We can discuss a useful first focus and explain available online lesson arrangements privately. Ask about practice, feedback and the initial review point before enrolling. The right decision is not that separate Physics always needs more tutoring or that Combined Science is automatically easier. It is support matched to the actual course and the specific difficulty preventing your child from working independently.
