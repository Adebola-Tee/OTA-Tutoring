SAT Advice

August SAT 2026 7-Day Study Plan for a 1400+ Score

By Taiwo Oloyede15 August 20266 min read

The August SAT is almost here.

If you are registered for the August 22, 2026 SAT and your practice score is not yet where you want it to be, the final seven days still matter.

This is not the week to learn everything on the SAT from scratch. It is the week to become much more deliberate about what you practise.

A student currently scoring around 1250, 1300 or 1350 does not necessarily need hundreds of random questions. You need to know where your marks are being lost, which mistakes are fixable and how to use the remaining days efficiently.

Here is a practical seven-day plan for the final week before the August Digital SAT.

First, know exactly what you are preparing for

The Digital SAT has two sections:

  • Reading and Writing
  • Math

Reading and Writing lasts 64 minutes with 54 questions, while Math lasts 70 minutes with 44 questions.

Both sections are divided into two modules, and your performance in the first module influences the difficulty of the second.

That adaptive structure is one reason your final preparation should include official digital practice rather than relying entirely on worksheets or old SAT materials.

Your goal this week is simple: find the areas costing you the most points and attack those first.

Day 1 — Take a full Digital SAT practice test

Start with a full-length practice test in Bluebook, the official application used for the Digital SAT.

Treat it seriously.

Sit somewhere quiet. Follow the official timing. Avoid checking answers midway through the test.

When you finish, don't focus only on the total score.

Ask yourself:

  • Which Math topics caused the most mistakes?
  • Were Reading and Writing errors caused by vocabulary, grammar or comprehension?
  • Did you run out of time?
  • Did you change correct answers unnecessarily?
  • Were your mistakes mostly knowledge gaps or careless errors?

Your score tells you where you are.

Your mistakes tell you what to do next.

Day 2 — Fix your weakest SAT Math areas

For many students aiming for 1400+, Math provides some of the most recoverable points.

Go through every Math question you missed on Day 1 and group the mistakes by topic.

You might find problems with:

  • linear equations
  • systems of equations
  • percentages and ratios
  • functions
  • quadratics
  • geometry
  • statistics
  • interpreting graphs
  • word problems

Do not spend the entire day practising topics you already answer correctly.

If eight of your recent mistakes came from algebra and only one came from geometry, your study time should reflect that.

Day 3 — Improve SAT Reading and Writing accuracy

Reading and Writing preparation should also be targeted.

Review your practice test and identify the type of questions you repeatedly miss.

Pay particular attention to:

  • grammar and punctuation
  • transitions
  • sentence structure
  • words in context
  • main ideas
  • evidence
  • conclusions
  • interpreting information from short passages and graphics

Do small, focused sets of questions rather than rushing through dozens without reviewing them.

For every wrong answer, ask:

Why is the correct answer correct, and why was my choice wrong?

That second question is important.

Reviewing your mistakes carefully is often more valuable than simply completing more questions.

Day 4 — Work on SAT timing

Sometimes the problem is not knowledge.

It is time.

You may understand algebra perfectly when practising at home but lose marks because you spend four minutes fighting one difficult question on the actual test.

Today, practise under time pressure.

Complete shorter timed sets from both sections and become comfortable moving on when a question is consuming too much time.

Within a module, you can return to questions before the module ends.

Use that strategically.

A difficult question should not steal time from three questions you could answer correctly.

Day 5 — Target your remaining weak areas

By Day 5, you should know your patterns.

Now narrow the focus further.

If punctuation is still costing you points, work on punctuation.

If advanced algebra remains shaky, work on advanced algebra.

If you repeatedly misread questions, slow down and practise identifying exactly what each question is asking before solving it.

The closer you get to test day, the less useful random studying becomes.

Precision beats volume.

Day 6 — Take one final full-length practice test

Take another full-length Digital SAT practice test.

Then compare it with your Day 1 performance.

Do not panic if your score does not suddenly jump by 100 points.

Practice scores can fluctuate.

Instead, look for evidence that your preparation is working.

Ask yourself:

  • Are you making fewer careless mistakes?
  • Is your pacing better?
  • Are previously weak topics becoming easier?
  • Are you reaching the end of each module with more control?
  • Are you recognising question patterns faster?

Those improvements matter.

If your score is still being held back by a few specific areas and you are unsure what to prioritise, individual support can help you use the remaining time more effectively.

Day 7 — Stop cramming

The day before your SAT is not the time for an eight-hour study marathon.

Do a light review.

Look through formulas, grammar rules or mistakes you have already corrected.

Make sure your testing device is ready.

Check your admission ticket and test centre information.

Pack what you need.

Then rest.

Students taking the August 22 SAT should also make sure their Bluebook exam setup is completed before test day.

Do not leave important preparation until Saturday morning.

Can you really reach 1400+ in seven days?

Possibly, but no responsible tutor can promise a particular SAT score in one week.

Where you finish depends heavily on where you are starting.

A student already scoring 1360 who is losing points through timing and a handful of recurring mistakes is in a very different position from a student currently scoring 1050 with major gaps across several topics.

The better question is:

How many points are you currently losing on mistakes you can realistically fix before August 22?

That is what this week should be about.

A 1400+ goal requires more than answering difficult questions.

It requires protecting the points available on questions you already have the ability to answer.

One week left means every SAT study session needs a purpose

Do not spend your final week collecting resources.

Do not keep changing study plans.

Do not take practice test after practice test without reviewing your mistakes.

Take one test. Diagnose the problems. Fix them. Practise under time pressure. Test yourself again. Then enter Saturday rested and prepared.
