GCSE Maths Tutoring

How to choose the right online GCSE Maths tutor for your child

By Taiwo Oloyede16 October 20264 min read

An online GCSE Maths tutor should be able to explain how their teaching would help your particular child. A broad promise to improve confidence is not enough information for a booking decision. Ask how they identify priorities, what the student does during lessons and how learning will be checked afterwards.

You do not need to know the whole syllabus to compare tutors thoughtfully. Bring a recent assessment and ask about the work in front of you. A clear answer should connect the student's attempt with a possible teaching priority, rather than immediately recommend a long package.

Confirm the qualification before discussing targets

Ask the school for the exact course, exam board and tier. For England's GCSE Maths, Foundation and Higher have different content demands and grade ranges. Our tier support guide explains why that matters. International GCSE is a separate qualification and needs its own check.

A tutor should know which materials fit the student's course. Pearson Edexcel's GCSE Maths qualification page is an official reference for that board. Other boards have their own specifications. The tutor should be comfortable confirming suitability rather than treating every paper with “Maths” on the cover as equivalent.

Listen to how a mistake is investigated

Consider this original example: solve 2(x + 3) = 18. A student writes 2x + 3 = 18, then x = 7.5. The first issue is distributing the multiplication, not dividing 15 by 2. Teaching should return to two copies of the whole bracket.

Expanding correctly gives 2x + 6 = 18, so x = 6. Substituting into the original expression confirms 2 × 9 = 18. Ask how the tutor would help the learner explain that correction and attempt a changed example independently. Repeating a rule without checking its meaning may leave the same error ready to return.

Ask what happens during an online lesson

Find out how the student shows written working, asks questions and attempts unfamiliar tasks. A shared screen can support explanation, but the tutor needs to see the student's thinking too. Watching a complete solution is different from choosing the first step without help.

Expect progress to be explained with evidence

Ask what the first learning goal might be and when it would be reviewed. A target such as solving equations with brackets independently can be checked with fresh questions. “Improve everything quickly” does not tell a parent what the tutor is planning or what would count as progress.

The EEF individual tuition review supports targeted teaching linked with classroom learning and monitored for impact. Research findings do not guarantee a private student's result. Ask how the tutor uses evidence to adjust the work when the current approach is not helping.

Check the relationship with school and home practice

The tutor should ask about current classwork and upcoming assessments. A plan can repair earlier knowledge while keeping the learner connected to school topics. Ask how independent practice will be chosen and what happens if the student cannot complete it without help.

Notice the student's experience too. Can they say they are confused without embarrassment? Do explanations become clearer when they ask? Feeling comfortable matters, alongside evidence that they can solve more on their own. A friendly relationship and purposeful teaching should support each other.

Understand the arrangement before agreeing

Discuss session length, frequency, fees, payment and cancellation terms before booking. Ask what is included outside the live lesson and how communication works. Our cost considerations guide helps you compare arrangements without assuming all quotes cover the same service.
