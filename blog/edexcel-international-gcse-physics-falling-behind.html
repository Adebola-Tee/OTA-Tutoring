IGCSE Physics Advice

Edexcel International GCSE Physics: What to Do When Your Child Starts Falling Behind

By Taiwo Oloyede22 September 20262 min read

When a student starts falling behind in Edexcel International GCSE Physics, each new topic can make the earlier gaps more visible. A weakness in graphs, forces, energy or electricity may reappear in several different questions.

The answer is not to restart the entire textbook. Begin with evidence.

Confirm the qualification and route

Pearson offers International GCSE Physics materials and different assessment routes. Use the specification followed by your child’s school. The official Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics page provides the relevant qualification documents and support materials.

Revision based on the wrong paper structure can waste valuable time.

Find the earliest repeated gap

Look through recent tests and homework. Do not ask only which chapter received the lowest score. Ask which skill keeps failing across chapters.

Common examples include:

  • rearranging equations and converting units
  • interpreting gradients and areas on graphs
  • distinguishing related physical quantities
  • explaining energy transfers precisely
  • applying principles to unfamiliar practical situations

Fix the underlying skill before adding more difficult questions.

Use short recovery cycles

Choose one concept, explain it clearly, complete a graduated question set and correct every mistake. Return to the same skill later with an unseen question.

A realistic week may include two weak-topic sessions, one calculation session and one mixed review. This is more manageable than an instruction to “catch up on Physics”.

Do not confuse watching with mastery

Worked solutions help students learn a method, but improvement must be checked without prompts. After guided practice, close the notes and attempt a new question independently.
