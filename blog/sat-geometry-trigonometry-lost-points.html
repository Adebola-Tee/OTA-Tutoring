How to Avoid Losing Points in SAT Geometry and Trigonometry

By Taiwo Oloyede6 October 20264 min read

A formula sheet does not decide which length is the radius, whether two triangles are similar or which side is opposite an angle. Students can remember geometry formulas and still lose points because the relationships in the diagram are unclear.

Start with one marked question and ask your child to redraw the figure, adding only information stated or established. The drawing should make the mathematical relationships visible without introducing assumptions about how the picture happens to look.

Identify the exact quantity

If a circle has diameter 10, its radius is 5. The area is therefore 25π, not 100π. The formula was never the main difficulty; the student used the wrong measurement.

Have them label the diameter and radius before substituting. Likewise, distinguish area from circumference and volume from surface area. Each describes a different quantity and uses different units.

A response in square centimetres is appropriate for area, while a length uses centimetres. Checking the units can reveal that the wrong kind of calculation has been performed.

Keep similarity ratios consistent

If two similar triangles have corresponding side lengths in a ratio of 2 to 3, their corresponding lengths scale by that factor. Their areas are in a ratio of 4 to 9, because two dimensions change.

A learner who applies the side ratio directly to area has missed the relationship between length and area. Use a simple pair of similar rectangles or triangles to demonstrate the scaling before returning to an exam question.

Mark corresponding sides carefully. Matching by where a side sits on the page can fail when one triangle is rotated. Angle correspondence and the established similarity should guide the pairing.

Define opposite and adjacent from the chosen angle

In a right triangle, the hypotenuse is opposite the right angle. The other side labels depend on which acute angle is being considered.

Take a right triangle with side lengths 3, 4 and 5. For the acute angle opposite the side of length 3, sine is 3 divided by 5 and cosine is 4 divided by 5. For the other acute angle, those ratios exchange roles.

Ask the student to point to the reference angle before naming a ratio. This prevents applying a memorised arrangement to every triangle, even when the angle changes.

Check whether a relationship applies

The Pythagorean theorem applies to right triangles. A diagram that looks nearly right angled does not establish that condition. Use the stated information rather than the visual impression.

Similarly, a formula is helpful only when the student identifies the relevant shape, dimensions and constraints. The official SAT Math overview shows where Geometry and Trigonometry fit into the assessed content.

Connect diagrams to equations

When a problem gives insufficient information for a claimed length, say so. Students should distinguish a relationship they can justify from an attractive guess based on the drawing. Asking what additional information would be needed can expose an assumption that otherwise remains hidden in the calculation.

Once lengths and relationships are identified, write the equation they imply. Our guide to SAT word problem setup offers a similar process for defining quantities before calculation.

Use fresh questions with figures drawn in different orientations. Ask the learner to explain why the method applies before calculating. Successful transfer shows more than completing a page of almost identical triangles.
