Yay, it is summer time and a lot of students have already planned how they intend to spend the next couple of weeks.

Many are enrolled in extracurricular activities while some intend to travel. Of course, the summer holiday is a good time to rest, spend time with family and enjoy other activities outside school.

However, taking a complete break from learning can take a toll on students when they resume in September.

There is already a new adjustment that comes with returning to school. It is a new year and a new phase. For students moving from Year 10 to Year 11, there is also the anxiety of knowing that this is the year they will sit for a major examination.

During the holiday, many students stop attending lessons and completely abandon personal reading. Important concepts and exam techniques are swept under the carpet. By the time school resumes, it becomes difficult to gather everything again.

Some have forgotten methods they understood before the holiday. Others struggle to remember key formulas or how to answer certain exam questions. It may then take several weeks to rebuild their understanding and even regain their confidence.

This is especially important for Foundation GCSE students.

Foundation students need consistency and it is not about intensity. That consistency can begin during the summer break.

It does not mean attending classes every day or studying for several hours. Even one hour of online tutoring each week for a subject of concern is enough to maintain momentum. The lesson can be relaxed and focused on revisiting important topics, correcting misunderstandings and keeping the student’s brain active.

Personal reading does not have to be stressful either.

A student may spend 20 minutes practising Maths and understanding important formulas. Another 20 minutes may be used for Science or English. There can also be time to review previous mistakes and understand why those answers were wrong.

The good thing about studying during the summer is that there is little pressure. There are no regular school activities, upcoming tests or several assignments to complete at the same time. This gives students the opportunity to read calmly and understand topics at their own pace.

They are not preparing under pressure. They are simply keeping their knowledge fresh and preparing themselves in advance.

There should still be a balance.

Students should enjoy their holidays, travel, spend time with family and take part in hobbies. However, completely neglecting education throughout the summer may make the return to school more difficult than it needs to be.

Year 11 already comes with its own pressure. Maintaining a simple learning routine during the summer can help students resume with better confidence and a stronger understanding of the topics they covered in Year 10.

It does not have to be intense.

One hour of tutoring each week and a few minutes of personal reading may be all that is needed to keep the momentum going.
